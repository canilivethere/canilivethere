Buenos Aires (Palermo focus) (Argentina)

Buenos Aires (Palermo focus), Argentina — promising; Community & social fabric is a strength, Nature & water-adjacency is the catch.

See the full score breakdown

Buenos Aires is a genuine world city dressed as a decades-older one — nearly 15 million people, block after block of extraordinary architecture, and more bookstores per capita than anywhere else on Earth. Palermo Soho is where the remote-work crowd actually lands, dense with cafés and coworking spaces, a short walk from Recoleta's grander, older-money quiet; Belgrano rounds out the neighborhood trio as the quieter, still-safe residential option. The climate runs humid subtropical rather than the "eternal spring" some neighboring capitals claim — hot, sticky summers, cool grey winters, no real dry season either way. And after decades of a currency story so volatile it needed its own vocabulary — the blue dollar, the cuevas, tourists bragging about a black-market exchange rate — that particular chapter closed in 2025: the official, informal, and market rates finally agree, and prices mean what they say.

A world capital with more bookstores per capita than anywhere on Earth — and a currency story that finally calmed down. — 25 bookstores per 100,000 people — the world's highest count, by a real margin over second place.

Overview
  • Currency-controls status
    Most capital controls (cepo cambiario) lifted April 2025, backed by a $20B IMF program — official, the electronic-payment exchange rate (MEP), and blue-dollar rates within 2-3% of each other as of early 2026, vs. 100%+ gap as recently as 2023
    The blue-dollar/cueva arbitrage is genuinely gone — a real, structural rules-of-the-game change. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Emergency care — access regardless of immigration status
    Yes
    Genuine emergencies are free for everyone, any immigration status, no exception found in any 2026-07-11 source (expatfinancial.com, budgetbucketlist.com, buenosairesherald.com all converge). Distinct from scheduled/non-emergency care, see next row. **No source_url added this pass (2026-07-18)** — three sources converge per the notes above but none was re-confirmed with a single stable URL this pass; flagged as a remaining gap.
  • Pet import (cat) — species-specific requirement verification
    Identical entry requirements to dogs — no species-specific divergence found in SENASA's own primary pages
    Checked directly 2026-07-14 rather than assumed — SENASA's own traveler-facing pages (the general entry page and the species/destination-requirements tool at mascotas.senasa.gob.ar) state the same International Veterinary Certificate/pet-passport, rabies-vaccination, health-certificate, and deworming requirements for cats as for dogs, with no titer and no quarantine if paperwork is in order (see the companion dog row for the full requirement list, identical for both species). The one genuine dog/cat asymmetry found: Buenos Aires city's (CABA) post-arrival dangerous-breed owner registry (Ley 4.078) is dog-only by its own definition — keyed to breed and physical characteristics (weight, chest circumference) that simply have no cat equivalent; no CABA cat registry, and nothing found suggests any other Argentine city runs one either.
  • Pet import (dog) — rabies titer / quarantine requirement
    No rabies titer (FAVN) required from any origin country; no quarantine if paperwork is in order
    Re-verified 2026-07-14 directly against SENASA's own traveler-facing pages (Servicio Nacional de Sanidad y Calidad Agroalimentaria, Argentina's national food-safety/animal-health agency), not aggregator paraphrase alone. Required regardless: an International Veterinary Certificate (CVI) or legalized pet passport valid 60 days from issuance (Spanish translation required per Resolución SENASA 1354/1994 unless an EU-style pet passport); rabies vaccination current, primary dose at least 21 days pre-arrival for animals over 3 months (age exception for pets from a WOAH-recognized rabies-free country); a health certificate dated within 10 days of the CVI's issuance; deworming within 15 days of the CVI. No import permit required for a pet as accompanied baggage; no national breed restrictions on entry. A real correction of a widely-cited aggregator claim: microchipping is NOT required to enter Argentina (pettravel.com claims otherwise, but neither of SENASA's own primary pages state an entry microchip requirement; microchips become relevant only for pets LEAVING Argentina for the EU or Uruguay). A pet without compliant paperwork faces quarantine (where a facility exists), return to origin, or euthanasia at the importer's cost (pettravel.com, not stated directly on SENASA's own page. Separate, location-specific, post-arrival finding NOT carried by this row: the City of Buenos Aires (CABA) runs a mandatory dangerous-breed owner registry (Ley 4.078) covering specific breeds including German Shepherd, Doberman, and Rottweiler — see the companion CABA fact (AR-buenosaires:caba-dangerous-dog-breed-owner-registry-ley-4078) for detail; this is a post-arrival municipal obligation, not an entry requirement, so it isn't folded into this row's own value.
  • Private healthcare quality
    Comparable to European standards, English-speaking staff at internationally-oriented private hospitals (Hospital Alemán, Hospital Italiano, Hospital Británico) — one of the least ambiguous positives found anywhere in this project
    Private insurance (prepaga — OSDE, Swiss Medical) runs ~$50-150/month. Care quality more uneven in Argentina's poorer northern provinces — not relevant to Buenos Aires/Bariloche/Mendoza specifically. **Relinked 2026-07-11 from `Safety` to `Healthcare quality & access`** now that the 13th criterion exists — Safety was the closest available home at original extraction time, not a miscategorization to correct blame for. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Private insurance (prepaga) — age-based rejection prohibition
    Yes
    Ley 26.682 — argentina.gob.ar's own plain-language explainer, checked 2026-07-11. Prepagas cannot reject an applicant for age or pre-existing conditions; every prepaga must offer its top age-bracket plan with no age ceiling, capped at 3x the entry-bracket premium; 10+ years at the same prepaga shields against further age-based increases past 65. **A real, checked exception to the pattern the 13th criterion's own definition assumed going in ("enrollment ceilings commonly 65-75")** — Confirmed-diverges only on the practice side: aggregator/journalism sourcing (iprofesional.com) describes real friction for retirees with pre-existing conditions despite the legal protection, not a first-person account. **No source_url added this pass (2026-07-18)** — the exact argentina.gob.ar explainer page URL wasn't re-located this pass; flagged as a remaining gap rather than guessed at.
  • Scheduled (non-emergency) public healthcare — free-access gate
    Free for national ID (DNI)-holding residents (temporary or permanent); billed for non-DNI holders (tourists, transitorios) in Buenos Aires city (CABA) + 4 other jurisdictions since March 2025
    Buenos Aires city (CABA) is the exact jurisdiction in play for Palermo/Recoleta/Belgrano. buenosairesherald.com + batimes.com.ar reporting, cross-corroborated by a baexpats.org community thread on the same policy. New restriction as of March 2025, not previously documented anywhere in this project
  • Temporary residents (DNI) — free public healthcare access
    No
    Decreto 366/2025 (May 2025): temporary-residence permit (DNI) holders retain healthcare access but must show private insurance to use it free at point of service. Reconfirms, doesn't contradict, visa-legal.md's existing Rentista-specific finding — a second independent source (corporateimmigrationpartners.com) checked this pass. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Monotributo (simplified self-employment tax regime) — category table
    11 categories (A-K), each with an annual gross-income ceiling and a fixed monthly fee; Category A ceiling ARS 10,277,988/year (monthly fee ARS 4,780), Category K ceiling ARS 108,357,084/year (monthly fee ARS 1,171,213)
    Confirmed directly against the official category table published by Argentina's federal tax agency (AFIP, renamed ARCA in 2024), checked 2026-07-18. A single flat monthly payment covers VAT, income tax, and social-security/health-insurance components together — Argentina's real, current, low-friction self-employment tax vehicle, distinct from the still-proposed 'Monotributo Tech' bill (see companion row above). Categories re-indexed periodically against inflation (the IPC) — these figures will drift, re-check before relying on them.
  • Monotributo — export-of-services provision (already in force)
    A Monotributista of any category can invoice a foreign client directly via a VAT-exempt export invoice (Factura E), in foreign currency or pesos, without upgrading to the more complex 'Responsable Inscripto' tax status
    A real, already-in-force mechanism sitting underneath (and separate from) the still-proposed 'Monotributo Tech' bill this file already documented — closes a real gap in this project's 'how does AI/remote income actually play out here economically and legally' research mandate. See the companion divergence-flagged row below for a real, unresolved primary-vs-secondary-source contradiction found on this same page.
  • Monotributo export income — counts toward the annual category ceiling?
    Yes (per AFIP's own primary text) — contradicted by secondary summaries citing Ley 26.565/27.618, which claim No
    Argentina's federal tax agency (AFIP)'s own export-of-services page, read directly, states a Monotributista 'pueden exportar servicios, sin superar los límites de facturación de la categoría máxima' (may export services WITHOUT EXCEEDING the maximum category's billing limits) — i.e. export income DOES count toward the ceiling. This directly contradicts a search-engine-summarized set of secondary sources (citing Ley 26.565 and Ley 27.618, and describing a 2025 reform eliminating a prior USD 36,000/year repatriation cap) that claimed export income does NOT count toward the ceiling. This pass could not resolve the contradiction — the primary page cites no specific law number to check against the secondary sources' citations. Flagged Confirmed-diverges between a primary source and secondary summaries on a real, load-bearing threshold question, not papered over. Worth a dedicated follow-up reading Ley 27.618's own text directly.

Visa & residency

How you'd actually get to stay — the real routes, their income floors, and how long they realistically take, not the marketing version.

  • Bienes Personales (wealth tax)
    Yes
    Annual wealth tax on worldwide assets above a threshold — a foreign brokerage account or a home back home comes into scope, not just Argentine holdings. source_url added 2026-07-18; see also the 2026 rate-reform detail (0.50-1.00% declining to 0.25% by 2027, per argentinavisalaw.com, checked 2026-07-18) added to overview.md's income-viability section.
  • Border-run (Colonia/Montevideo ferry) enforcement status
    Discouraged-but-still-largely-functional as of mid-2026, not yet an active crackdown — a rising-risk gradient, not a hard stop
    Decree 366/2025 (May 2025) gave migration authorities explicit authority to reject entry on suspicion a declared purpose doesn't match real intent. BAExpats forum texture: first 1-2 runs typically go through fine, subsequent runs described as "a gamble," more friction for people with prior extensions on record. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Citizenship by birth (ius soli) — status after Decreto 366/2025
    Yes
    A child born on Argentine soil to foreign parents is still automatically an Argentine citizen at birth, 'sea cual fuere la nacionalidad de sus padres' (whatever the nationality of their parents) — Ley 346 Article 1's own current text, fetched directly and confirmed unchanged. Re-derives and corrects an inherited claim that Argentina 'ended unconditional birthright citizenship' — that claim is wrong on its face; what actually changed is a separate, related mechanism (see the companion parent-of-citizen-child residency fact below). Divergence flag Not yet checked because this pass verified only the statutory text, not whether Registro Civil practice issues citizenship documents to these children at the same rate post-2025 amid the broader immigration crackdown.
  • Citizenship-by-investment track (separate, new, 2025)
    Media-reported possible $500,000+ threshold — separate track, does not modify or replace Article 23(d)'s existing route
    A presidential emergency decree (DNU) 366/2025 plus Decreto 524/2025 (published July 31, 2025). No official figure published as of a January 2026 check — aimed at a much wealthier investor tier. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Citizenship residency requirement
    2 continuous years of legal residency with zero interruptions of any kind
    Decree 366/2025 eliminated the previous practice of maintaining eligibility through brief trips abroad — a materially stricter rule than "2 years" reads on its face. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Digital Nomad visa accepts passive income as qualifying proof
    No
    Requires foreign-sourced remote-work income (freelance clients, foreign employer, or foreign-registered online business) — mirrors Colombia's active-income-only framing. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Digital Nomad visa category
    Transitory (non-resident) visa, explicitly not a residence category
    Decree 366/2025 formalized this. Up to 180 days, renewable once (360 days/one cycle total) — does not lead to permanent residence under any circumstances, same trap as Colombia's equivalent. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Inversionista minimum capitalization
    1000–1200 $ (ARS 1,500,000 statutory figure)
    **Corrected 2026-07-08**: previously stated as $30,000-60,000, an aggregator-conflation error. Two independent sources (luxbrumalis.com.ar, June 2025; goldenharbors.com, May 2026) converge on ~$1,000-1,200 — the ARS figure was fixed in 2010 and never updated against ~350-400x currency devaluation since. source_url (Decreto 616/2010's own text via Infoleg) added 2026-07-18.
  • Inversionista real approval gate — discretionary review
    'Interés para el país' standard historically 'never defined,' operating with high discretion — likely the real gate, not the nominal dollar figure
    An Argentine immigration lawyer's own practice page describes it this way; independently corroborated by the Argentine government's own stated rationale for building a separate 2025 citizenship-by-investment track, calling this existing regime "eroded by discretion, trapped in a bureaucratic maze". source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Inversionista sector/scale restriction
    No
    Confirmed directly against Decreto 616/2010's own text via Infoleg (Argentina's official legislative database), not aggregator paraphrase — the same check that caught Morocco/Taghazout's wrong score. No sector, business-type, or scale restriction appears anywhere in the operative legal language. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Inversionista small-business precedent
    Named approved cases: a French national's textile-manufacturing venture, and food-service/restaurant ('gastronómicos') ventures
    Secondhand via a practicing immigration lawyer's own case experience, not a first-person forum post. First lived-account evidence found that a genuinely small, owner-operated business has cleared this approval process. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Inversionista visa category
    Temporary residency via active business investment (Ley 25.871 Art. 23(d), Decreto 616/2010), leading to permanent residency after 3 years
    "Whoever contributes their own assets to carry out activities of interest to the country" — a real residency pathway, not a Visitor-equivalent dead end. source_url (Ley 25.871's full current text via Infoleg) added 2026-07-18.
  • No US-Argentina income tax treaty
    Yes
    Same gap Colombia has. FEIE doesn't help passive income at all — Foreign Tax Credit is the primary tool, contingent on Argentine tax being paid first. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Overstay penalty (exit authorization)
    ~28 $ (ARS ~40,000, habilitación de salida fee)
    Administrative fee-and-exit process, not currently criminal. Valid for 10 consecutive days to depart. Decree 366/2025 removed the informal warning-first grace this used to run on. source_url added 2026-07-18 (primary argentina.gob.ar page confirmed live).
  • Parent of an Argentine-citizen child — residency status
    Capped temporary residency (max 3 years), converting to permanent only after 2 years and only if economic-means and clean-record conditions are met — previously automatic, direct permanent residency before Decreto 366/2025 (May 29, 2025)
    The actual mechanism the 'birth tourism' pattern exploited: old Article 22 of Ley 25.871 granted automatic permanent residency to 'parientes de ciudadanos argentinos... entendiéndose como tales al cónyuge, hijos y padres' (spouse, children, AND PARENTS of Argentine citizens) — so a foreign parent of an Argentine-born (automatically-citizen) child qualified for automatic PR with no wait. Decreto 366/2025's new Article 23 inciso ñ) replaces this with capped temporary residency for 'cónyuge, progenitor o hijo' (spouse, PARENT, or child) of a citizen, max 3 years, converting after 2 years only if the same economic-means/clean-record conditions apply project-wide post-2025. Identical mechanism to the already-documented spousal reunification finding, just the parent-of-citizen-child branch of the same Article 23 inciso ñ) clause.
  • Parent-of-citizen-child residency rule — nationality-neutral in statutory text vs. Russian-specific enforcement framing
    Yes
    Article 22/23 inciso ñ)'s own text names no nationality — applies identically to a parent of any nationality. The political/enforcement context diverges sharply: Argentina's own ambassador (quoted directly by EADaily) named ending 'maternity tourism... from Russia' as the reform's purpose, citing 'more than 23,000 Russians arrived in Argentina between 2022 and 2023' — corroborated by multiple 2025 Latin American press sources (La Tercera, SoyArmenio, a24.com, the last describing three pregnant Russian nationals detained at Ezeiza on suspicion of misrepresenting travel purpose). Confirmed-diverges: a neutral law text with a real, dated, nationality-specific enforcement pattern and political framing behind it — the political-transition-watch convention's exact target shape.
  • Pensionado visa converts to permanent residency
    Not yet checked
    Every source describes Rentista and Pensionado as 'sister programs under the same legal framework' with comparable paths to permanent residency — structurally plausible (same law, same decree) but not independently traced to Pensionado's own primary-text conversion terms the way Rentista's confirmed 2-year clock is. Worth a follow-up primary-source check before treating this as confirmed identical to Rentista's clock, not just analogous. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Pensionado visa duration
    1 year, renewable (one aggregator source describes a possible up-to-3-year grant under the same Disposición 3446/2023 — unresolved discrepancy, not independently re-verified against primary text)
    Sourced identically to Rentista's own renewal shape via the official service page for the 1-year figure; the up-to-3-year reading traces to the same regulatory instrument but is a search-result characterization, not independently re-verified. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Pensionado visa income threshold
    1300-2000 $/month
    Low end ($1,300) is the statutory 5x the national minimum wage (the SMVM) threshold; high end ($2,000) is the practitioner-recommended buffer. Identical statutory threshold to Rentista's own — 5x SMVM (ARS 1,815,000/month at mid-2026's ARS 363,000 SMVM, ~$1,300/month at this file's ARS/USD convention). Multiple practitioner sources recommend budgeting closer to $2,000/month in practice to absorb peso volatility and clear reviewer scrutiny — the same $2,000/month working figure this file already uses for Rentista, not a separate number for the sister category. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Pensionado visa income-type requirement (passive)
    explicitly_rejected
    Ordinary investment/dividend income is explicitly not this route's qualifying basis; Rentista (the separate route) is the fit for ordinary asset-return passive income instead. See companion pension-type row. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Pensionado visa income-type requirement (pension)
    primary_accepted
    A genuine employment-linked or state pension, paid 'regular and permanent' by a government, international organization, or private company, for services rendered abroad. Same structural pattern as Colombia's Pensionado route. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Pensionado visa legal basis
    Ley 25.871, Article 23(c) — a textually distinct provision from Rentista's Article 23(b), same Decreto 616/2010 and a National Directorate of Migration resolution (Disposición DNM 1.732/2023, as modified by Disposición 3446/2023)
    Confirmed via direct fetch of argentina.gob.ar's own official service pages for both categories side by side — a genuine second, dedicated pensioner-specific route distinct from Rentista, not Rentista stretched to cover pension income. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Permanent-residency abandonment threshold
    12 consecutive months abroad (reduced from 24)
    Decree 366/2025 — spending a year outside Argentina after gaining PR now risks losing it, half the previous grace period. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Progressive income tax top rate
    35 % (residents)
    Argentina's tax authority was renamed to the Revenue and Customs Control Agency (ARCA, formerly AFIP) in 2024 — same function. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Property purchase confers residency status
    No
    Ley 25.871 has no "property owner" visa category — Inversionista requires founding/personally managing a business, not triggered by a real-estate purchase itself. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Re-entry ban for misrepresented entry purpose
    5 years
    The sharpest concrete penalty number found. No first-hand BAExpats account found of an ordinary repeated visa-runner (vs. someone using fake documents) actually receiving the full ban rather than a same-day refusal — "graduated risk, severe worst case rarely realized in practice but real". source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Reciprocity fees
    No
    Ended in stages (US removed March 2016, Australia July 2017, Canada January 2018) — nobody currently pays a reciprocity fee to enter as a tourist. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Regime shape
    open-freehold

    Temporary residency, evolving toward permanent residency

    Yes

    Residency-based, not territorial; triggers at PR grant or ~12 months physical presence (also see the separate 183-day tourist-time trigger above)

Property

Can you buy here, and what it actually takes to do it — ownership rules, structures, and real price bands, not listing-site optimism.

  • Boleto de compraventa does not convey ownership
    Yes
    Only the registered escritura pública (public deed) conveys ownership and priority over other claims — described as the most common wrong assumption foreign buyers make. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Tax ID (CDI) requirement to close a purchase
    Required from Argentina's tax authority (AFIP/ARCA) — described as the single document that trips up more foreign buyers than any visa or residency issue
    People don't learn about it until closing. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Condominio (undivided co-ownership) legal regime
    Código Civil y Comercial Articles 1983-2036 (enacted 2014, effective 2015) — same underlying shape as Guatemala's/Colombia's proindiviso, Mexico's copropiedad en indiviso, just a different name
    Each co-owner holds an undivided share; ending the arrangement without every co-owner's agreement requires a judicial división de condominio (court-supervised partition). **No source_url added this pass (2026-07-18)** — a primary Infoleg/Código Civil y Comercial text link wasn't located this pass; flagged as a remaining gap rather than an unverified guess. See the companion Vines of Mendoza / BAExpats rows below (added 2026-07-18) for new supporting evidence on whether this mechanism actually gets used by foreign buyers in practice.
  • Propiedad Horizontal (formal condominium-unit regime)
    Ley 13.512, now Civil and Commercial Code Articles 2037-2072 — each unit gets its own separate, individually-titled deed
    Standing recommendation: set up Propiedad Horizontal (or subdivide) at time of purchase rather than buying jointly under condominio and sorting titles out later. **No source_url added this pass (2026-07-18)** — same gap as the companion condominio row above; a primary Infoleg text link wasn't located this pass.

    Complex title issues, inheritance situations, or border-zone approvals can extend this by several months. source_url added 2026-07-18.

Red flags

The hard truths, stated plainly — real risks, sitting right next to everything that's actually going well.

  • 2026 debt maturities
    19–20 $ billion
    Currency stabilization's durability depends on the Central Bank's ability to keep building reserves — not yet a settled, durable equilibrium. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Argentina-wide dangerous-region equivalent to Colombia's conflict zones
    Not yet researched
    Not specifically searched for this pass — a real gap before calling Argentina uniformly safe outside Buenos Aires
  • Business closures / job losses since Milei took office
    2,000+ businesses closed, ~73,000 jobs lost, concentrated in manufacturing
    The backdrop the "currency arbitrage is gone" headline sits on top of, not a separate story. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Dollar-vs-peso rental divide
    Documented case: a peso-earning Argentine professional couple lost their apartment to a dollar-driven price hike; a foreign digital nomad's own on-record 'insane' framing of the dynamic
    Buenos Aires Times reporting. The one live thread that does connect to foreigners specifically — individual-level resentment/awareness, not organized action. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Gentrification backlash — no organized foreigner/nomad-targeted pattern found
    No evidence of organized protest or explicit anti-foreigner/anti-digital-nomad messaging in Buenos Aires, any neighborhood, any year searched
    Reads closest to Oaxaca's quiet-displacement pole, not CDMX's organized marches or Medellín's poster campaigns. Confidence Medium — this pass lacked Spanish-language local press/social media access. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Homicide rate (Palermo/Recoleta/Belgrano)
    3–5 per 100,000
    Comparable to or better than mid-sized US cities (well below Chicago, Philadelphia, New Orleans). Belgrano specifically ~70% lower crime than the city's southern neighborhoods. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Immigration policy direction
    Tightening, not loosening — Decree 366/2025 cut PR-abandonment window, imposed zero-interruption citizenship clock, reclassified Digital Nomad as non-resident — even as the same government economically courts foreign remote-income earners (Monotributo Tech bill, 'non-human corporation' proposal)
    A real, named tension: economically courting this population while administratively tightening the rules around it. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Inflation
    20–30 % (estimated, 2026)
    Down from extreme highs but still genuinely high by normal-country standards; monthly inflation ticked up for 5 consecutive months as of early 2026. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • October 26, 2026 midterm election result
    Milei's La Libertad Avanza won 40%+ nationally, 15 of 24 electoral districts — calmed markets, strengthened his governing hand
    A real, direct signal distinct from and more informative than the separate ~35% approval-rating figure. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Petty theft — the actual common risk
    Phone snatching specifically, called out repeatedly for Palermo Soho around Plaza Serrano
    Not violent crime or home invasion — the honest, common risk in the neighborhoods actually in play. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Real economic displacement — La Boca (documented, pre-dates nomad era)
    1,100+ residents displaced in La Boca in 2016 alone (96 eviction trials that year); 54.5% of a sampled cohort no longer at their 2000 address by 2008
    Driven by state real-estate "Economic District" policy and judicial eviction, not documented as foreigner/digital-nomad specific. source_url added 2026-07-18.
  • Real wages (formal sector, Feb 2026 vs. Nov 2023)
    -9 % (inflation-adjusted change)
    Currency stabilization has come with a real, measurable cost to ordinary Argentines' purchasing power. source_url added 2026-07-18.
