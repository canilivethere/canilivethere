Visa & residency

How you'd actually get to stay — the real routes, their income floors, and how long they realistically take, not the marketing version.

  • Border-zone property restriction for foreigners
    ~100km border-proximity scrutiny zone (mechanics read less bright-line than Mexico's)
    Source's own text: "worth a property lawyer's confirmation if a specific parcel is ever near a border"
  • Tax authority (DIAN) foreign-digital-platform tax-reporting rule
    Yes
    KPMG, Baker McKenzie, PwC, Thomson Reuters taxathand. Platform operators (Airbnb, Uber, delivery apps, marketplaces) must report their Colombian tax-resident sellers'/hosts' transactions to Colombia's tax authority (DIAN) starting Q4 2025 data (due Feb 27, 2026) — does not create any new personal tax obligation on a foreign digital nomad's own foreign-sourced remote-work income, and does not change the existing 183-day trigger. Trade-press "digital nomad tax surveillance" framing significantly overstates who this targets
  • Digital Nomad Visa converts to permanent residency
    No
    Visitor(V)-category (Resolución 5477 de 2022) — time on it does not count toward the 5-year Migrant residency clock. Divergence flag closed 2026-07-08: the rule is stated unambiguously in statutory text, but no lived applicant account confirming this in practice was found; applies identically to Rentista
  • Digital Nomad Visa income-type requirement (passive)
    explicitly_rejected
    Foreign active income only — remote employees, freelancers/contractors with foreign clients, or digital entrepreneurs; explicitly excludes Colombian-sourced income; nothing found suggests passive/investment income qualifies.
  • Digital Nomad Visa mandatory private "all-risk" health insurance, incl. repatriation
    Yes
    Ordinary public health-insurance (EPS) enrollment does not satisfy this visa requirement — a private/international policy is required, tightened 2026-07-09 to explicitly require medical-repatriation coverage (ordinary travel insurance no longer accepted). A genuine compound gate against the age-cutoff row above: someone past a local insurer's cutoff needs an international/travel product with a higher or no age cap to keep this visa route open. Not yet checked whether Pensionado carries the same explicit mandate
  • Digital Nomad Visa rejection-rate (reported tightening)
    20–30 % (reported rejection-rate increase; alt. stat: 58% approval rate, 2,918 applications rejected in 2025)
    Nearly every source repeating this traces back to a handful of trade-press/SEO-aggregator articles (travelandtourworld.com's own headline literally reads "Explosive Digital Nomad Visa Crisis"); the one quantified figure traces to Medellín Guru's own 2025 report (credible, but not a government statistic). Real, reported administrative tightening that this pass could not independently verify against a primary government data source
  • Digital Nomad Visa (Visa V) income threshold
    1400 $/month
    3x the national monthly minimum wage (SMMLV) = COP 5,252,715/month. Strict "no averaging" — each of the last 3-6 months' bank statements must individually clear the threshold, not average out to it
  • M-Investor Visa converts to permanent residency
    Yes
    Migrant(M)-category — counts toward the residency clock, unlike Rentista
  • M-Investor Visa income-type requirement (passive)
    explicitly_rejected
    An asset/investment test (verifiable capital placed in a vehicle: 350 or 650 SMMLV), not an income test of any kind — passive income alone, without the qualifying capital placement, does not satisfy this route. Same shape as Guatemala's Investor Visa and Thailand's Long-Term Resident visa (LTR) Wealthy Global Citizen category.
  • M-Investor Visa — real estate investment threshold
    155000–165000 USD
    350x the national monthly minimum wage (SMMLV) ≈ COP 612,816,750
  • Migrant (M) Work Visa — employer/contract route
    Colombia-based employment contract or service agreement; valid up to 3 years, multiple entries; counts toward the Resident (R) visa, generally after 5 years
    Only relevant with an actual Colombia-based contract — documented for completeness
  • No US-Colombia income tax treaty
    Yes
    Only a Tax Information Exchange Agreement (data-sharing) exists — double-taxation relief relies entirely on US domestic mechanisms (Foreign Tax Credit; FEIE doesn't help passive income at all, since it isn't "earned" income under IRS rules)
  • Non-resident tax rate (under 183 days)
    35 % flat, Colombia-sourced income only
    Foreign income, US investments, etc. aren't taxed while non-resident — exposure only kicks in once residency triggers
  • Path to Permanent Residency (R visa) — standard timeline
    5 continuous years on a qualifying Migrant (M) visa (drops to 2 years for spouse/partner/parent of a Colombian national, qualifying investment, or a Mercosur visa)
    Clock is genuinely continuous — a lapsed/unrenewed visa between cycles resets it. R visa cancelled if >2 continuous years outside Colombia; Cédula de Extranjería requires a transfer-procedure renewal every 5 years even without leaving. Note the 2-year figure was itself corrected 2026-07-09 to a 2-3 year honest range for the marriage/family route specifically — see Option 6.
  • Pensionado Visa converts to permanent residency
    Yes
    Migrant(M)-category, counts toward the standard 5-year residency clock
  • Pensionado Visa income threshold
    1375–1400 $/month
    3x the national monthly minimum wage (SMMLV) = COP 5,252,715/month. Migrant(M)-category — this one does count toward the 5-year residency clock, unlike Digital Nomad or Rentista
  • Pensionado Visa income-type requirement (passive)
    not_stated_by_source
    Pension/retirement-account income (Social Security, employer pension, IRA/401(k) systematic withdrawals) is primary_accepted — see companion pension-type row. Ordinary (non-pension) passive income is not_stated_by_source: a directional read, not a citation to a stated rejection.
  • Pensionado Visa income-type requirement (pension)
    primary_accepted
    Pension/retirement-account income is the route's real qualifying basis.
  • PIP extension and cumulative annual cap
    One 90-day extension (prórroga de permanencia/PTP); 180 days total per calendar year (Jan 1–Dec 31), fixed calendar reset, not a rolling 12-month window
    Exits/re-entries do not reset the cumulative count — border runs are structurally pointless past 180 days/year, independent of enforcement mood. Extension cost ~$22-40 (COP 85,000-150,000), ~3 business days processing
  • PIP overstay penalty
    90–3300 $ (fine, officer discretion)
    Two reporting frameworks (26.31-210.50 UVT vs. 0.5-7 SMLMV) diverge at the low end (~$367 vs ~$233) but converge near the top (~$2,900-3,300); a one-day overstay also informally reported at a flat ~$93. Repeated/serious violations carry re-entry-ban risk (up to 5 years generally)
  • Proyecto de Ley 238 de 2025 — pending rural/agricultural foreign-ownership cap
    Not yet law as of mid-2026 — would cap foreign ownership of rural agricultural-frontier land at 15% of a municipality's agricultural-frontier land, bar foreigners from more than one Family Agricultural Unit, and prohibit foreign acquisition of baldíos/reserve areas/land within 50km of borders
    Rural/agricultural only — would not affect an urban apartment purchase in Medellín or Santa Marta, but would matter for a coffee-region finca. Trajectory qualifier per source: "worsening (if passed)" — not yet law as of this writing, so the worsening direction is conditional on passage
  • Regime shape
    open-freehold
    The existing 'Restricted Zone applies = No' row stays the underlying confirmed fact this derives from.
  • Rentista Visa converts to permanent residency
    No
    Reclassified from Migrant to Visitor(V) category by Resolución 5477 de 2022 — a real structural downgrade relative to Guatemala's Rentista (direct-to-permanent). Divergence flag closed 2026-07-08 — same "Not yet checked" reasoning as the Digital Nomad Visa route above
  • Rentista Visa income threshold
    4585–4670 $/month
    10x the national monthly minimum wage (SMMLV) = COP 17,509,050/month — more than 3x Guatemala's equivalent Rentista threshold ($1,250/month)
  • Rentista Visa income-type requirement (passive)
    primary_accepted
    Rental contracts, dividend/annuity statements, and investment account records all named as qualifying evidence alongside bank statements.
  • Resident progressive tax rate range (2026)
    0–39 % of worldwide income
    Filing required if gross income exceeds 1,400 times the tax-value unit (UVT) (~COP 69.7M/year) or gross assets exceed 4,500 UVT
  • Restricted Zone (coastal/border) applies to ordinary residential property
    No
    Colombia's Constitution guarantees foreigners the same civil property rights as citizens; no fideicomiso-equivalent, unlike Mexico's 50km coastal/100km border restricted zone. Confirmed independently for both Medellín/Cartagena and Santa Marta/Bocagrande in property.md
  • What IS restricted (equally for citizens, not foreigner-specific)
    Indigenous resguardos, national parks, other collectively-held/protected land, and the coastal bajamar intertidal zone
    Off the private market for anyone — distinct from Mexico's rule, which restricts foreigners specifically from ordinary coastal land, not just the beach itself
  • Restricted-nationality list (tourist/visitor entry, PIP visa-free tier)
    IL
    Israel removed from Colombia's visa-free entry list via Resolution 8558/2025 (reciprocity after Israel required visas of Colombian citizens, following the Petro government's 2024 diplomatic rupture over Gaza) - Israeli citizens now need an advance Visitor (V) Visa, not a PIP stamp on arrival. A reciprocal removal was agreed 2026-07-15 (Colombia/Israel foreign ministers, restoring full diplomatic relations, embassy in Jerusalem) tied to the 2026-08-07 change of Colombian administration - real, dated, high-confidence prospect of reversal, but not yet in force as of extraction. Sources: Fragomen/Kan-Tor & Acco Law Firm alert (2025-08-11, citing the resolution), Vialto Partners regional alert (resolution number/date independently confirmed), colombiareports.com (2025-06-06 announcement), El Universal/La Silla Vacia/El Nuevo Siglo/El Heraldo/El Colombiano (2026-07-15, reciprocal removal agreement). Re-checked 2026-07-17 ([specialist], desk-search-recheck per this project's own worked example in [on file] §2): fresh search finds no change — still agreed-but-not-in-force, implementation still tied to the 2026-08-07 handover, multiple Colombian outlets (El Heraldo, El Nuevo Siglo, Cambio Colombia) confirm the same status one day later, nothing reversed early.
  • Restricted-nationality list (V-TU visa required in advance instead of visa-free PIP entry)
    CN, IN, RU
    A pre-existing, non-diplomatic-reciprocity tier (predates the 2025 Israel-specific action above, which is a separate carve-out) -- nationals of China, India, Russia and (per source) 'most African and Middle Eastern countries' must obtain a V-TU (Visitante-Turismo) visa in advance rather than arriving on a PIP stamp; valid 365 days, multi-entry, each individual stay still capped at 90 days. This CN/IN/RU triplet is the confirmed, named floor -- the fuller African/Middle Eastern membership is NOT enumerated here since ISO-code enumeration requires named confirmation per country, not a demonym/regional description ([on file] Section 8H.2); treat this list as under-complete, not exhaustive. Extracted 2026-07-17 ([specialist], cross-candidate nationality-library pilot, priority nationality: Russian) from prose already on file (colombianized.com, 2026), re-checked this pass via a fresh search cross-corroborating affirmalegal.com/visacolombianaparaextranjeros.com/holasim.com -- unchanged. Direct fetch of Cancilleria's own tramites/visa pages returned HTTP 403/404 this session; not yet reached at the primary-source tier. Turkish/Ukrainian/Venezuelan nationals are NOT on this restricted list -- they get the standard visa-free PIP entry.
  • PPT/ETPV (Venezuelan temporary protection status) converts to permanent residency
    Yes
    The single largest nationality-specific migration program of any candidate in this project, previously entirely unresearched in this file. After 5 years' continuous PPT (Permiso por Proteccion Temporal) possession, or 5 years combined PEP+PPT time, holders become eligible to apply for the Visa R under the ETPV -- a dedicated Resident-visa track for this population. PPT itself currently carries validity through 2031-05-30. Sourced to help.unhcr.org/colombia (directly quoting the statute's own conversion language: 'La tenencia ininterrumpida del PPT durante 5 anos habilita a los titulares para solicitar la Visa R para venezolanos bajo el ETPV'), cross-corroborated by independent legal-registry listings of Decreto 216 de 2021 (Funcion Publica's Gestor Normativo, SUIN-Juriscol, Refworld) agreeing on decree number/date/vigencia.
