  • Digital Nomad Visa documentation shape
    Proof that the qualifying income is earned from clients or employers outside Ecuador (the work must be for foreign entities), plus health insurance valid in Ecuador for the visa's full duration. The income bar itself is the same as the Rentista/Pensioner routes — what distinguishes this route's paperwork is proving the income is active foreign-sourced work rather than passive.
  • Professional Visa documentation shape
    A bachelor's degree from an accredited university, registered with Ecuador's credential-recognition agency (SENESCYT) — and that registration is its own real gate: it costs roughly $550-1,200 and takes 3-6 months before the visa application can lean on it. The income bar this route then requires is far lower than every other Ecuadorian route, which is exactly why the credential step carries the weight.
  • Investor Visa documentation shape
    Proof of the qualifying investment held in Ecuador — real estate, a bank certificate of deposit, or a business — with no monthly income proof required at all: the investment itself is the entire financial test, a genuinely different documentation burden than the income-stream evidence every other Ecuadorian route demands.

Community

Who else lives here, how you'd actually meet them, and what it's like once the novelty wears off.

  • Established foreign-resident population
    8000–10000 people
  • Room for others / group viability
    Nascent, not operational — two real named Cuenca-area examples in planning/exploratory stage, set against a genuinely low current co-living baseline and a named cultural stigma
  • Subjective community read — gentrification/gringo-pricing critique
    Systematic overcharging of foreigners at some vendors; local rent-affordability squeeze plausibly linked to expat demand

Red flags

The hard truths, stated plainly — real risks, sitting right next to everything that's actually going well.

  • Cannabis law
    Recreational personal use illegal since Executive Decree No. 28 (Nov 24 2023); medical legal since a Sept 2019 National Assembly vote (83-23)
  • Compound gate — visa-application insurance proof required before cédula/IESS eligibility exists
    Yes
  • Executive-judicial tension
    Yes
  • IESS coverage scope
    Full network coverage, no copays/deductibles; pre-existing conditions covered after a 3-month wait; dental + prescriptions included
  • IESS voluntary affiliation — foreigner eligibility gate
    Requires legal residency (cédula); tourist-visa holders cannot enroll
  • IESS voluntary affiliation monthly cost
    84.83–264 $/month
  • Kidnapping/cartel-extortion risk specifically targeting foreigners (national)
    Express kidnapping and 'vacunas' (protection-money extortion) disproportionately affect tourists/foreign organizations per OSAC-sourced advisory material, concentrated in Guayaquil and coastal trafficking corridors; national extortion cases overall rose from 1,616 (2019) to 23,080 (2024) before a reported 30% drop to 16,131 (2025), though the drop is contested as under-reporting rather than real decline
  • National homicide rate
    51 per 100,000
  • National state of internal armed conflict (declared Jan 2024)
    Yes
  • November 2025 constitutional referendum — rejected on all 4 questions
    60.75% No (foreign military bases); 58.20% No (end public party funding); 53.59% No (reduce Assembly seats); 61.71% No (new Constitution)
  • Pet import (cat) — species-specific requirement verification
    Same regime as dog, plus FVRCP
  • Pet import (dog) — rabies titer / quarantine requirement
    No quarantine with paperwork in order; Agrocalidad import permit ~$30, 1-2 weeks to process
  • Private health insurance — new-enrollment age ceiling
    Commonly cited 60–70; not reconciled to one figure age in years
  • WHO health-system ranking — corrected vs. a circulating expat-marketing claim
    111th of 211 countries (2000) improved to 20th (2014) rank
  • Complex/tertiary care referral pattern
    Stabilized locally, referred to Quito/Guayaquil (short domestic flight via Mariscal Lamar airport) for specialist/tertiary care; full international medevac an option some plan for the most severe cases
  • Distance to emergency care
    In-city — major public university-affiliated hospital + private 24/7 ERs
  • Homicide rate (Cuenca-specific)
    1.4 per 100,000
  • Internet speed (median fixed-line download)
    93 Mbps
  • Kidnapping/cartel-extortion risk specifically targeting foreigners (Cuenca)
    No documented Cuenca-specific incident pattern found; national extortion/kidnapping surge is real but reporting concentrates targeting on locals, businesses, and transit workers, with foreigner-targeting patterns (express kidnapping, vacunas) documented at the national/Guayaquil level, not confirmed for Cuenca specifically
  • Local IESS hospital quality, relative
    Cuenca's own IESS hospitals rated 'generally better equipped' than IESS facilities in smaller Ecuadorian cities
  • Curfew status
    No
  • State of emergency / state of exception status
    Yes
  • Structural/natural-hazard risk (seismic/volcanic)
    Medium hazard classification (ThinkHazard) — 10% chance of potentially-damaging earthquake shaking in the next 50 years
  • Homicide count trend, Jan-May 2025 vs Jan-May 2026
    Rose from 3 (Jan-May 2025) to 18 (Jan-May 2026) — a 6x year-on-year increase over the five-month window
