Antigua (Guatemala)

Antigua, Guatemala — promising; Climate is a strength, Nature & water-adjacency is the catch.

See the full score breakdown

Antigua was Guatemala's capital until an earthquake ended that arrangement in 1773, and the city never entirely got over the demotion: cobblestone streets, a UNESCO-protected colonial core, and three volcanoes standing watch over every rooftop view. Sitting at 1,530 meters keeps the air spring-like all year — no real winter, no real summer, just the same mild register morning after morning. It's also Guatemala's best-established foreign-resident town by a wide margin, with Spanish schools doubling as social clubs and a genuinely thriving coworking scene, forty-five minutes from the capital's airport and its best hospitals. Call it the country's easy mode: a well-worn corridor rather than a frontier, which is exactly the tradeoff worth weighing in the chapters below.

Guatemala's easiest on-ramp — a colonial city three volcanoes still watch over. — Elevation 1,530m — the reason it never really has a summer or a winter.

  • Digital Nomad Visa documentation shape
    Bank statements demonstrating the required foreign income received over the prior year (a higher flat bar applies with dependents), proof the income is stable and foreign-sourced, proof of NO Guatemalan income and no employment relationship with any Guatemalan entity, and international health insurance. No Guatemalan guarantor is required — a friction point older categories had. Since 2026, Guatemala's immigration authority (IGM) also conducts a mandatory field visit to verify the applicant's actual address as part of processing.
  • Rentista / Pensionado Visa age gate
    0 years
  • Rentista / Pensionado Visa documentation shape
    An apostilled criminal background check issued within the last 2 years (a 2025 easing — previously 5), proof of the qualifying passive income (pension, rental income, investment income, or dividends; US Social Security is among the most commonly accepted sources), and standard biographical documents. Filing is in person at the foreigners' office (Subdirección de Extranjería) of Guatemala's immigration authority (IGM) in Guatemala City, and every apostille is far easier obtained in the home country before traveling. Once granted, the income must be re-proven every 5 years.
  • Investor Visa age gate
    Not yet researched
  • Investor Visa documentation shape
    Documented proof of the qualifying investment, verifiable and shown to have arrived from abroad by bank transfer. Most commonly real estate: a notarized public deed (escritura pública) registered in the national property registry (Registro General de la Propiedad) — unregistered property does not count, and multiple properly-documented properties can be combined to reach the bar. Business capital, an earmarked bank deposit, or purchase of an existing business also qualify. Processing includes a mandatory field-verification step in which an inspector from Guatemala's immigration authority (IGM) confirms the investment and the applicant's address actually exist; realistic notarial/legal assistance costs sit on top of the official fees.

Red flags

The hard truths, stated plainly — real risks, sitting right next to everything that's actually going well.

  • President Arévalo — 2023 election mandate and prosecutorial backlog under Porras
    Won over 60% of the vote in the 2023 presidential election on an anti-corruption, reformist platform; under outgoing Attorney General María Consuelo Porras, over 93% of criminal cases — including organized-crime cases — remained unaddressed as of early 2026
  • 2026 institutional reset — judicial/oversight body appointments
    Mixed record: TSE new magistrates finalized by Congress, confirmed in place by 2026-04-29 (exact swearing-in date not confirmed); Constitutional Court reappointment of Roberto Molina Barreto on 2026-03-07 (a real setback for reform); Attorney General replacement of María Consuelo Porras with Gabriel Estuardo García Luna, in office since 2026-05-17 (a real institutional win); Comptroller General nominating-commission representatives elected 2026-08-06, commission installed by 2026-08-12, new Comptroller takes office 2026-10-13
  • January 2026 national state of emergency / state of siege
    Declared January 18, 2026, a 30-day 'state of siege' after suspected gang members killed seven police officers in Guatemala City, following prison riots in three facilities where gang-affiliated inmates had taken roughly fifty hostages

    Congress approved the final version June 2, 2026; enters into force ~September 2026; Guatemala's bar association reportedly considering legal challenges
