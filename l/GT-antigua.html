Antigua (Guatemala)

Antigua, Guatemala — promising; Climate is a strength, Nature & water-adjacency is the catch.

See the full score breakdown

Antigua was Guatemala's capital until an earthquake ended that arrangement in 1773, and the city never entirely got over the demotion: cobblestone streets, a UNESCO-protected colonial core, and three volcanoes standing watch over every rooftop view. Sitting at 1,530 meters keeps the air spring-like all year — no real winter, no real summer, just the same mild register morning after morning. It's also Guatemala's best-established foreign-resident town by a wide margin, with Spanish schools doubling as social clubs and a genuinely thriving coworking scene, forty-five minutes from the capital's airport and its best hospitals. Call it the country's easy mode: a well-worn corridor rather than a frontier, which is exactly the tradeoff worth weighing in the chapters below.

Guatemala's easiest on-ramp — a colonial city three volcanoes still watch over. — Elevation 1,530m — the reason it never really has a summer or a winter.

  • Territorial tax system — foreign-sourced income exempt
    Yes
    Guatemala runs a territorial tax system — only Guatemala-sourced income is taxed; foreign-sourced income (remote client work, pension, investment income from abroad) is exempt from Guatemalan income tax. Tax residency triggers at 183+ days/year; once a tax resident, the practical step is an annual declaration flagging the foreign-source portion as exempt, process overhead rather than a tax bill. This is the load-bearing fact behind the Income viability (AI/remote fit) criterion's 4/5 score at both Antigua and Atitlán, extracted for the first time this pass — the criterion was previously scored with zero backing fact rows in this file despite real, well-sourced reasoning already sitting in visa-legal.md's prose
  • Remote work performed while physically in Guatemala — tax-source gray area
    Legally unresolved; not currently enforced against digital nomads with foreign clients
    There is an unresolved legal debate about whether labor physically performed in Guatemala for a foreign client counts as "Guatemala-sourced" income despite the territorial system above. In practice, digital nomads/remote AI workers with foreign employers or clients are not being pursued for Guatemalan tax on that income — but "not currently enforced" is explicitly not the same claim as "definitively legal." This is the specific fact behind why this criterion isn't scored a clean 5/5 despite the territorial system's real strength — a genuine, named soft edge rather than a settled protection.
