  • Digital Nomad Visa documentation shape
    Bank statements demonstrating the required foreign income received over the prior year (a higher flat bar applies with dependents), proof the income is stable and foreign-sourced, proof of NO Guatemalan income and no employment relationship with any Guatemalan entity, and international health insurance. No Guatemalan guarantor is required — a friction point older categories had. Since 2026, Guatemala's immigration authority (IGM) also conducts a mandatory field visit to verify the applicant's actual address as part of processing.
  • Rentista / Pensionado Visa age gate
    0 years
  • Rentista / Pensionado Visa documentation shape
    An apostilled criminal background check issued within the last 2 years (a 2025 easing — previously 5), proof of the qualifying passive income (pension, rental income, investment income, or dividends; US Social Security is among the most commonly accepted sources), and standard biographical documents. Filing is in person at the foreigners' office (Subdirección de Extranjería) of Guatemala's immigration authority (IGM) in Guatemala City, and every apostille is far easier obtained in the home country before traveling. Once granted, the income must be re-proven every 5 years.
  • Investor Visa age gate
    Not yet researched
  • Investor Visa documentation shape
    Documented proof of the qualifying investment, verifiable and shown to have arrived from abroad by bank transfer. Most commonly real estate: a notarized public deed (escritura pública) registered in the national property registry (Registro General de la Propiedad) — unregistered property does not count, and multiple properly-documented properties can be combined to reach the bar. Business capital, an earmarked bank deposit, or purchase of an existing business also qualify. Processing includes a mandatory field-verification step in which an inspector from Guatemala's immigration authority (IGM) confirms the investment and the applicant's address actually exist; realistic notarial/legal assistance costs sit on top of the official fees.

Recent change events

2026-09-10 — Four Guatemalan income-type refusals withdrawn. No verdict on the board changes. visa severity 1

Four claims about Guatemalan residency routes are withdrawn: that the Digital Nomad route rejects investment income, rejects pension income, and rejects income earned working locally, and that the Investor route rejects investment income.

Three cite a private Guatemala guide site. One cites the immigration authority's own published residency regulation -- the right document, but not one we had retrieved and read at the point the claim was recorded.

These rows all said the same kind of thing: that a particular visa route will not accept a particular kind of income -- a pension, investment income, or money earned working remotely for clients abroad. That is a hard "no" to a reader, and a hard no is exactly the sort of claim that stops someone applying for a route they might actually qualify for.

When we went back to check where each of these refusals came from, we could not show it. In every case the citation points at a website rather than at a page we have actually retrieved and read saying so. That does not make the claims wrong. Several of them are probably right. It means we cannot demonstrate them, and we are not willing to leave a hard no standing on a source we have not opened.

So these rows are held rather than deleted. They render nothing and count for nothing until someone opens the source page and confirms what it says. If it turns out to say what we claimed, the row comes straight back unchanged.

What a reader sees change: no verdict on this board moves. This is separate from the September sourcing-label corrections for Guatemala; those changed how well claims were cited, this withdraws four claims outright.

2026-09-09 — Thirteen Guatemala cost-of-living figures showed the date we wrote them down, not the date the figure was true. No price has changed — only the dates beside them. cost severity 1

Every price and budget figure on this site carries a date. The date on a fact is when the figure was true or the rule took effect. Where we have re-checked it, the check date is shown too. On thirteen Guatemala cost-of-living entries the date shown was the day we typed the entry in — a check date sitting in the field that says when the figure was true.

Ten are Antigua entries — four rent bands for the historic centre plus one rate someone reports actually paying, two restaurant-meal tiers, a single-person monthly budget, one resident's own lived monthly spend, and a couple's short-stay ledger. Three are around Lake Atitlán: a Panajachel café-coffee band, a Panajachel restaurant main-dish band, and a two-bedroom rent band for San Pedro La Laguna.

Nine of the thirteen now show the date their own source carries, and in most cases that is older than what we had been displaying: six of the Antigua rent and budget figures come from pages last updated in April 2025, and three rent bands are dated to March 2026, which is the month their publishers say their figures describe. Where a source gives both a vintage for its figures and a later date on which the page itself was edited, the vintage is the one that belongs beside the number, and that is the choice made here. The remaining four are the more uncomfortable part: their sources carry no date at all — two are merchants' own posted menus, two are guide pages with no publication line — so those entries now say plainly that no date is stated, with the day we checked them shown beside that, instead of a date we had supplied ourselves.

The part worth being blunt about is that fixing this required no research. Every correct date was already written into our own note on the same entry, by the same person who entered it, on the same day. We had made this a rule for ourselves back in July — a figure's date does not move just because we looked again — and then broke it. This is a failure to record something we already knew, not a failure to find it out.

This is not the same as the two Guatemala date corrections published on 6 September, and it should not be read as correcting them twice. Those were two specific one-day errors inside individual facts — a court reappointment and a swearing-in — where the event's own date was wrong. Here nothing about any fact's content is wrong and no figure has moved; what was wrong is the date stamp shown beside each figure, which is what tells a reader how old a price is.

We are not claiming the problem is finished. A mechanical test for the same defect flags about forty further entries across ten countries as possibly carrying it — some of those will turn out to be legitimate, because a figure's real date genuinely can be the day it was read. And that test can only see entries that carry a record of having been checked, so an entry with the same problem and no such record is invisible to it. The true size of this is not known. What is fixed today is these thirteen, and three more in Marrakech corrected alongside them.

2026-09-07 — We showed a lake-warming rate as Lake Atitlán's water-quality reading. The source we cited does not contain that number, and we have removed the entry. other severity 1

Our Lake Atitlán entry headed "Lake cyanobacteria/water-quality problem" gave, as its value, a warming rate: +0.34 °C per decade. We got two things wrong, and the second one we had already spotted ourselves.

We cited NASA's Earth Observatory page on the harmful algae bloom in Lake Atitlán. We have now retrieved that page and searched its bytes directly. The figure is not on it — not "0.34", not "warming", not "temperature", not "decade", nowhere. The page does describe the cyanobacteria bloom, and does name untreated sewage, agriculture and deforestation among its causes, so it supports what we say about the blooms themselves. It simply never carried the number we hung on it. Our own source list for the lake's water quality names two further sources, a research paper and a magazine article, and we have never retrieved either. So there is no source we can point to that carries this figure.

The second problem is that it was the wrong kind of answer. A warming rate is a possible cause of algae blooms; it is not a measure of whether the water is safe to swim in. Our own review of this site on 10 July 2026 said exactly that about this exact entry — that it reduced the water-quality problem to a warming number and dropped the health-relevant content. Nobody acted on it, and the number stayed on the page from that day until now.

We have removed the figure from the page and struck the claim from the research note behind it. We are not saying the figure is false. We are saying we cannot show a source for it, and that it was answering the wrong question. What stays is the substance: the recurring toxic-algae blooms, the untreated sewage, agricultural runoff and deforestation-driven erosion behind them, the presence of E. coli and Giardia, and the joint NASA/USAID bloom-forecasting dashboard that monitors the lake. Lake Atitlán's nature-and-water score is unchanged at 3 — the reasoning behind that score always rested on the blooms and the sewage, never on the warming figure. This was the only fact we had filed under nature and water-adjacency for Lake Atitlán, so that heading now shows no facts on file for the location. That is accurate, and we would rather show nothing there than a number we cannot stand behind.

2026-09-06 — We dated a Guatemala Constitutional Court appointment and a Comptroller-commission swearing-in from the day their news coverage published, not the day they happened. Both are fixed, one day off each. stability severity 1

A Guatemala fact row stated Congress reappointed Constitutional Court magistrate Roberto Molina Barreto on 2026-03-07, and that the Comptroller General's nominating commission was installed by 2026-08-12. Both dates were wrong by one day, from two related but distinct errors: the first read a news article's publish date as the date of the event it reports; the second carried a scheduled date forward as though it had been confirmed.

The Constitutional Court date was sourced to a Tico Times article published 2026-03-07 -- but the article's own text says Molina Barreto "was selected Friday," and 2026-03-06 is the Friday; 2026-03-07 is a Saturday. Prensa Libre, timestamped 2026-03-06 03:33h, reports the vote count directly and is the real source for the date: 2026-03-06. The Comptroller-commission date had a related but distinct problem -- "installed by 2026-08-12" was a scheduled date, carried forward as though it had been confirmed to happen on schedule. Infobae's 2026-08-13 report of the actual swearing-in is the real date.

Both dates are now corrected everywhere they appeared. The wrong Constitutional Court date had actually been recorded twice in the same entry -- once as the date itself and once repeated in the explanation of where that date came from -- and both instances are fixed, not just the first. A related, previously-open sourcing note on the same row (the Attorney General's name and start date, resting on three Wikipedia pages rather than a press source) is also closed out: a direct press citation for that pairing has been found and is now cited.

The sharpest fact here is the mechanism, not the one-day gap: the article cited for an event's date says the event happened the day before the article was published. That is worth watching for elsewhere -- a publish date is not an event date, and a scheduled date is not a confirmed one.

2026-09-06 — Ten Guatemala facts and four visa routes carried sourcing labels stronger than what we can actually show. The underlying data mostly checked out; the labels didn't. other severity 1

Five further rows -- Guatemala's AML/beneficial-ownership reform taking force, the 2026 judicial-appointment reset, and the Category A/B/C nationality lists' own full enumeration -- were labelled "more than one source, cross-checked" on the strength of press reporting, law-firm commentary, and Wikipedia agreeing with each other. The same nationality-list claims also live in a second table -- the one describing entry routes rather than individual facts -- and four rows there move as well: Guatemala's three visa categories (visa-exempt, consular visa, consulted visa) and the CA-4 free-movement arrangement with El Salvador, Honduras and Nicaragua.
