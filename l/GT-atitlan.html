Overview
  • Public social-security health system (IGSS) closed to Digital Nomad/Rentista foreigners
    Yes
    Guatemala's national social-security health system's (IGSS's) enrollment is employer-triggered only (employer must enroll any worker within 20 days of hire — confirmed directly via livinginguatemala.com's own 2026 IGSS guide, exact page path not recorded in sources.md, domain-level citation); self-employed/freelance/passive-income foreigners fall outside the structure entirely. Both the Digital Nomad and Rentista/Pensionado visas explicitly require no Guatemalan-employer relationship, so IGSS is structurally closed to holders of exactly the routes most likely to be used here, independent of income level. The same finding applies identically at Atitlán

    65–260 $/month

  • Pet import (cat) — species-specific requirement verification
    Same no-titer/no-quarantine framework as dogs, different core-vaccine list
    Same paperwork chain as dogs — microchip, rabies vaccination, health certificate, an import permit from Guatemala's agriculture ministry (MAGA), and USDA endorsement — no titer or quarantine either; the only species-specific difference found is the required core-vaccine panel (cats: panleukopenia, rhinotracheitis, calicivirus, leukemia, vs. dogs' distemper/hepatitis/leptospirosis/parvovirus).   • Pet import (dog) — rabies titer / quarantine requirement
    No rabies titer required; no quarantine if compliant
    No rabies titer (FAVN/RNATT) test required regardless of origin country, no quarantine when paperwork is in order — despite Guatemala itself being classified high-rabies-risk by WOAH (the titer requirement concerns origin-country risk classification, not Guatemala's own). Real paperwork still required: ISO 11784/11785 microchip before vaccination, rabies vaccination 21-30 days pre-travel, health certificate within 14 days, core vaccinations (distemper/hepatitis/leptospirosis/parvovirus), parasite treatment, an import permit from Guatemala's agriculture ministry (MAGA), USDA endorsement for US-origin pets. Net: more paperwork steps than a no-vaccine-check destination, but no titer/quarantine puts this toward the easier end of this project's pet-import comparisons.   • Territorial tax system — foreign-sourced income exempt
    Yes
    Guatemala runs a territorial tax system — only Guatemala-sourced income is taxed; foreign-sourced income (remote client work, pension, investment income from abroad) is exempt from Guatemalan income tax. Tax residency triggers at 183+ days/year; once a tax resident, the practical step is an annual declaration flagging the foreign-source portion as exempt, process overhead rather than a tax bill. This is the load-bearing fact behind the Income viability (AI/remote fit) criterion's 4/5 score at both Antigua and Atitlán, extracted for the first time this pass — the criterion was previously scored with zero backing fact rows in this file despite real, well-sourced reasoning already sitting in visa-legal.md's prose
  • Remote work performed while physically in Guatemala — tax-source gray area
    Legally unresolved; not currently enforced against digital nomads with foreign clients
    There is an unresolved legal debate about whether labor physically performed in Guatemala for a foreign client counts as "Guatemala-sourced" income despite the territorial system above. In practice, digital nomads/remote AI workers with foreign employers or clients are not being pursued for Guatemalan tax on that income — but "not currently enforced" is explicitly not the same claim as "definitively legal." This is the specific fact behind why this criterion isn't scored a clean 5/5 despite the territorial system's real strength — a genuine, named soft edge rather than a settled protection. No Guatemala-specific AI-sector program, incentive, or restriction was found in any pass to date (checked again this session against livinginguatemala.com's digital-nomad content, freakingnomads.com, and digitalnomads.world — none mention AI-specific work, a local tech/AI scene, or AI-targeted policy) — flagged as a genuine research gap, not silently assumed absent

    ~35% approval; 13 impeachment petitions and 6 attempts to lift his immunity since taking office (2024); attorney general widely seen as hostile to his reform agenda
    Matters more for "will the rules change under me" (visa/tax/property law) than for personal safety day-to-day
  • 2026 institutional reset — judicial/oversight body appointments
    Mixed record: TSE new magistrates (Mar 2026, "amid corruption fears"); Constitutional Court reappointment of Roberto Molina Barreto (Mar 2026, a real setback for reform); Attorney General replacement of María Consuelo Porras with Rafael García Luna (May 5, 2026, a real institutional win); Comptroller General appointment pending as of Aug 2026
    One real win (AG) and one real loss (Constitutional Court) so far — not a verdict either way. Five key oversight/judicial bodies being renamed within a five-month window in 2026 — either a real reform opportunity or a volatile pressure point depending how it goes. Tico Times: Constitutional Court outcome "deals a blow to Arévalo's push for judicial reform", with accusations the US embassy lobbied on the reappointed magistrate's behalf
  • January 2026 national state of emergency / state of siege
    Declared after 10 police officers killed in coordinated gang attacks; lifted after about a month, but a nationwide "state of prevention" remains in effect
    Six months old as of this research, not ancient history. Distinct from the personal-safety/crime axis — named here because a national security-emergency declaration is itself a rules-of-the-game/institutional-capacity signal, not just a crime data point
  • AML law rewrite (Decreto 15-2026) — legislative passage
    Congress approved the final version June 2, 2026; enters into force ~September 2026; Guatemala's bar association reportedly considering legal challenges
    A real, dated example of Guatemala's rules of the game changing on a near-term timeline (property-transaction due-diligence/reporting obligations), not a hypothetical future risk — the property-mechanics detail lives in the land-property-access-tagged row above, this row is the political-process/institutional-change fact
  • Inter-town hiking-trail robbery risk
    Yes
    Use chartered boats between villages instead — trails not easily reachable by emergency services
  • Kidnapping/cartel-extortion risk specifically targeting foreigners
    Unverified single mention
    One visitor account mentions "regular extortion from locals," unverified beyond that single mention — flagged as something to watch for corroboration, not established fact
  • Lake cyanobacteria/water-quality problem
    +0.34 °C/decade warming
    2010–2024 warming trend; NASA/USAID runs an active bloom-forecasting dashboard — a monitored, not solved, problem
  • Foreign/outside ownership share
    ~90 %
    Only ~10% of vacation homes/eateries/hostels/luxury stays remain Maya-owned — the sharpest version of a dynamic also visible, less extremely, in San Pedro/San Juan.   • Planned power outages — frequency
    Roughly monthly in some areas, for maintenance
    "Not a crisis, but a real, recurring fact of life, not a one-off inconvenience" per the source file's own framing — one of the two concrete day-to-day frictions (alongside potable water below) the scorecard's own Routine sustainability/pace row for Atitlán (3/5) weighs against the lake's slower, quieter pace.   • Potable tap water access
    No — bottled/purified water delivery is the norm
    20L jugs, roughly 5 GTQ each — a small recurring cost and errand, not free-flowing infrastructure. Contrasts with Antigua, where no equivalent caveat is documented in this project's research to date
