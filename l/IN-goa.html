  • Employment (E) Visa converts to permanent residency
    No
    No time-based or status-based conversion exists from the Employment Visa (or any work/business visa) to permanent residency (PR). India's only PR-adjacent mechanism, the Permanent Residency Status (PRS) scheme (Ministry of Home Affairs, launched 31 Aug 2016), is a wholly separate track gated on foreign direct investment -- Rs 10 crore (~US$1.2-1.5M) within 18 months OR Rs 25 crore (~US$3-3.75M) within 36 months, plus generating employment for at least 20 resident Indians every financial year -- not on holding or extending an Employment Visa. An Employment Visa holder with no independent qualifying investment has no route into PRS. Absent PRS-level investment, an Employment Visa is a renewable-but-capped track only: extendable year-to-year by the local immigration registration office (the FRRO/FRO) up to a total of 5 years from initial issuance (confirmed at IN:employment-visa-renewability, this same file), with no further conversion after that ceiling -- a genuine structural dead end for this route, distinct from Mexico's temporary-to-permanent 4-year on-ramp (MX:temporary-resident-visa-converts-to-permanent-residency) and closer in shape to a hard wall. Trajectory read as stable: scheme unchanged since its 2016 launch, no reform proposal found this pass. Cross-corroborated across an Indian embassy source (indembastana.gov.in, reporting the MHA/PIB 2016 scheme announcement) and multiple independent aggregator/law-firm sources (expatorbit.com, mondaq.com, taxnews.ey.com, expatfocus.com) all converging on the same eligibility shape; direct fetch attempts against mha.gov.in and pib.gov.in primary PDFs (prsforeigninvestors_17042017.pdf, the PIB press release, and the MHA work-visa FAQ PDF) all returned HTTP 403 this pass -- the embassy source is the closest reachable primary-institutional citation, not a first-choice aggregator paraphrase. Closes our records (converts_to_pr was the sole remaining data_gap cause on this route).
  • Tax residency — 182-day rule and RNOR-to-ROR transition
    Resident if present 182+ days/year (or 60+ days current year + 365+ days across preceding 4 years). A Resident is further RNOR (India-sourced income only) if an NR in 9 of the preceding 10 tax years, or present ≤729 days across the preceding 7 tax years — typically the first 2-3 tax years of continuous stay. Once neither RNOR condition holds, the person becomes ROR and India taxes worldwide income, including foreign-client income never remitted to India.
    A real, previously-undocumented income-viability constraint for anyone genuinely settling in Goa rather than tourist-visa-hopping: after roughly 2-3 consecutive years at 182+ days/year, RNOR protection lapses and worldwide income (including remote/freelance earnings from foreign clients, paid into a foreign account and never brought into India) becomes taxable in India. Not independently cross-checked against India's own Income Tax Act text or incometaxindia.gov.in this pass — PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries is a reputable professional-advisory source but a single one. Paper-vs-practice divergence: Not yet checked — no first-hand account found of enforcement against a foreign resident's undeclared foreign-client income specifically.
  • DTAA double-taxation relief network
    India has 94 comprehensive Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAAs) plus 8 limited-scope treaties, using credit and exemption methods to prevent double taxation of the same income; a unilateral Section 91 relief mechanism covers residents whose home country has no DTAA with India at all. Claiming relief requires filing/documentation with Indian tax authorities, not automatic.
    Directly relevant once a foreign national in Goa transitions from RNOR to ROR status (see IN:tax-residency-182-day-rule-and-rnor-to-ror-transition) and India begins taxing worldwide income — relief exists via treaty but is a filing obligation, not a passive exemption. Whether the reader's own home country has a DTAA with India, and its specific terms, is nationality-dependent and not surveyed here. Aggregator/law-firm-blog tier source, not independently verified against India's own Income Tax Department DTAA list (incometaxindia.gov.in) this pass.
  • Individual income tax slab rates — new regime, FY2026-27
    0% up to ₹4 lakh; 5% ₹4-8 lakh; 10% ₹8-12 lakh; 15% ₹12-16 lakh; 20% ₹16-20 lakh; 25% ₹20-24 lakh; 30% above ₹24 lakh. Section 156 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 (successor provision to Section 87A under the repealed 1961 Act) rebate zeroes tax liability up to ₹12 lakh taxable income under the new regime (₹5 lakh under the old regime). ₹ lakh/year (top marginal rate 30% above ₹24 lakh, ≈$27,000-28,500 depending on exchange rate)
    Slab brackets unchanged from FY2025-26 per multiple sources (bajajhousingfinance.in, cleartax.in). CORRECTED 2026-08-24 ([specialist], responding to [specialist] citation-integrity audit task): originally cited as 'Section 87A' — that is the OLD 1961 Act's numbering for this rebate, no longer current law under the new Income Tax Act, 2025 (effective FY2026-27). Re-fetched bajajhousingfinance.in directly and confirmed it actually attributes the rebate to 'Section 157,' never mentioning 87A at all — the original fact's own section number didn't even match its cited source. Further research found bajajhousingfinance.in's '157' is itself wrong: Section 157 of the new Act covers relief for salary received in arrears/advance, an unrelated provision (confirmed via CAclubindia's 'Section 87A and Section 156 Rebate Explained: Why Tax May Still Be Payable Below Rs 12 Lakh,' which explicitly distinguishes Section 156 from Section 157). The correct section is 156, confirmed via strong cross-corroboration across independent tax-advisory sources: ClearTax ('Section 156 Income Tax Act 2025-Tax Rebate Explained'), Axis Max Life, ICICI Prudential Life, TaxGuru, Godrej Capital, eztax.in ('Section-156: Rebate of income-tax in case of certain individuals'), AUBSP ('Income Tax (No. 2) Act 2025: Section 156'), and TaxTMI's direct comparison piece 'Clause 156 of the Income Tax Bill, 2025 Vs Section 87A of the Income Tax Act, 1961.' Section 156 has three sub-sections: (1) old-regime rebate, 100% of tax or ₹12,500 whichever is less, income ≤₹5 lakh; (2) new-regime rebate, 100% of tax or ₹60,000 whichever is less, income ≤₹12 lakh (per eztax.in's quoted provision text), with marginal relief above ₹12 lakh; (3) caps the sub-section-2 deduction at the tax payable under Section 202(1)'s rate schedule. A search-indexed incometaxindia.gov.in page is titled 'Section 156 - Income Tax,' consistent with this — but direct fetch of incometaxindia.gov.in returned 403 (bot-blocked) both attempts, so this was not read against the government's own primary text directly this pass; treat as cross-corroborated across independent secondary tax-advisory sources, not primary-institutional-confirmed.
