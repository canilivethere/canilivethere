Marrakech (Morocco)

Marrakech, Morocco — a stretch; Infrastructure & connectivity is a strength, Nature & water-adjacency is the catch.

See the full score breakdown

Marrakech is Morocco's clear answer to a digital-nomad hub, though a much smaller and more tight-knit one than its reputation might suggest — the country's most developed coworking scene, anchored by a well-known Gueliz space, and a social calendar built around a rotating Thursday-night meetup rather than an anonymous expat sea. Housing ranges from modern Gueliz apartments to traditional medina riads, two genuinely different ways of living in the same city. Summers run properly hot and dry, regularly 40-45°C in the afternoon, while winters stay mild by day and cool at the edges — spring and autumn are the town's best-kept secret, climate-wise. And getting here has gotten easier fast: Marrakech's airport now reaches 108 destinations across 26 countries, with fares starting as low as $23 one-way since a major airline opened its first African base here in 2026.

  • Carte de Séjour pour Inactif documentation shape
    A Moroccan bank certificate (attestation bancaire) showing sufficient funds, 3-6 months of bank statements, an apostilled clean criminal-record check, a Moroccan medical certificate, and proof of accommodation (lease or title deed).   • Political Stability Index (World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators)
    -0.44 index (-2.5 weak to +2.5 strong; 2024 figure, up from -0.53 in 2023)
    TradingEconomics/World Bank WGI; sits somewhat below the world average of -0.07 — a genuinely middling, slightly-improving baseline, not an outlier-low score
  • Dirham currency-peg transition (mid-float, 2026-2027 target)
    Bank Al-Maghrib widened the trading band from 0.3% to 2.5% (2018) then 5% (2020, still pegged to a 60% euro/40% dollar basket); 2026 pilot phase for inflation-targeting, full float/effective implementation targeted late 2026/early 2027
    FXTradingAfrica, DabaFinance, Bloomberg, MoroccoWorldNews, citing Governor Abdellatif Jouahri directly. Over 80% of Moroccan companies reported as too small to be prepared for real exchange-rate risk — the central bank's own stated reason for moving cautiously rather than on the IMF's preferred faster timeline. A real, deliberate, gradual transition to watch, not a currency crisis or abrupt devaluation risk
  • Foreign-investment/capital-controls posture — IGOC 2026 reform
    Instruction Générale des Opérations de Change (IGOC) 2026, effective Jan 1 2026 — the most significant reform to Morocco's capital-controls regime in years; resident foreign investors holding investments 10+ years can transfer up to MAD 2M/year (~€185,000) abroad without original proof-of-financing documents; individual travel/transfer allowances raised across the board
    Primary source: the Office des Changes (Morocco's own foreign-exchange regulatory authority) — its own publication announcing IGOC 2026, corroborated by Lawyard, AMERELLER, MoroccoWorldNews, NorthAfricaPost. Sits on top of the 2022 Investment Charter, still in active rollout. No tightening, expropriation threat, or foreign-ownership rule reversal found anywhere — the confirmed direction of travel for foreign capital specifically is opening, not closing
  • 2023 Al Haouz earthquake exposure
    Yes
    Magnitude 6.8 (fact previously said 6.9 — minor, within normal early-vs-revised reporting variance), ~72km from Marrakech, ~2,900-3,000 dead mostly in Atlas villages; five-year reconstruction underway (~$11.7B), real but incomplete progress. 2026-08-25 verification review: original citation (emro.who.int) returned HTTP 502 on repeated re-fetch this session — WHO regional pages get reorganized and the URL may simply have moved, not proof it never existed — but since it can't currently be checked, swapped the source_url to Britannica (independently corroborated the same magnitude/death-toll/distance figures via CNN and Gulf News too). Substance confirmed, citation corrected.
