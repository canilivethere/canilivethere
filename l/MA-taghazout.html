Overview
  • Complex-surgery/oncology national referral tier
    Casablanca (Cheikh Khalifa International, Mohammed VI University Hospital)
    Marrakech's own private hospital (CIM) and public university hospital (Mohammed VI CHU) are strong for a secondary city but not the national top tier; real referral line for the most complex cases likely runs to Casablanca — an inference from where capability concentrates, not a confirmed Marrakech-specific medevac policy
  • Kidnapping/cartel-extortion risk specifically targeting foreigners
    Not yet researched
    Not found in either Marrakech's or Taghazout's own research — no keyword hit for this mechanism anywhere in Morocco's files, distinct from the already-checked "no organized anti-nomad backlash" community finding
  • Pet import (cat) — species-specific requirement verification
    Not yet researched
    Whether cat rabies-titer timing or quarantine requirements differ from the dog findings above has not been checked
  • Distance to emergency care
    20–30 minutes (to Agadir)
    Only a local health centre exists in Taghazout itself; real emergency/hospital-level care (AKDITAL Agadir International, Hôpital Hassan II) is a 20km drive — meaningfully shorter than Atitlán's 2.5-3 hour reference point this project uses
  • In-town hospital-level care (system capability, distinct from distance)
    No
    No hospital-level facility at any tier sits in Taghazout itself — only a local health centre and private practitioners for minor ailments; all real hospital-level options (AKDITAL, Hôpital Hassan II, Polyclinique de la CNSS) are in Agadir. Kept as its own Healthcare-criterion row, separate from the Safety-linked distance row above, per the criterion's own exclusion of travel-time from its score
  • Local Taghazout-specific crime rate
    Not yet researched
    Unlike Marrakech's own Numbeo crime/safety index, no location-specific figure was found for Taghazout
  • Air connectivity — Agadir Al Massira Airport (AGA, ~25-30 min away)
    73 destinations, 28 airlines
    Led by Ryanair, easyJet, and Jet2; 10 German destinations, Lisbon/Porto, Barcelona, a new Berlin route from June 2026, and one long-haul route to Montreal (Air Transat, ~8h15m). A real, genuine positive that partly offsets Taghazout's weak local-internet finding — flight access to Europe is not meaningfully worse than Marrakech's, even though internet is.
Visa & residency

How you'd actually get to stay — the real routes, their income floors, and how long they realistically take, not the marketing version.

  • Carte de Séjour pour Inactif converts to permanent residency/naturalization
    Yes
    Naturalization eligibility after 5 years continuous legal residence — a real, if slower, on-ramp; not confirmed whether any of Morocco's 8 permit categories has a Colombia-style dead end
  • Carte de Séjour pour Inactif income threshold
    1000–1650 $/month
    No single published figure — Decree 2-09-607 leaves it to case-by-case assessment, roughly €1,000-1,500/month per the sources cross-checked
  • Carte de Séjour pour Inactif income-type requirement (passive)
    not_stated_by_source
    Not confirmed how passive (non-pension) income generally would be classified; the 20% flat-rate carve-out applies to certain employment income specifically, not confirmed to extend to passive income. Decree 2-09-607's 'proof of means' language is silent on income type, not just amount.
  • Diligence risk — melkia vs. titre foncier (national mechanism)
    Yes
    National mechanism; severity genuinely differs by location — see the two location-scoped rows below.
  • Pet import (dog) — rabies titer / quarantine requirement
    Titer required (>0.5 IU/ml, 30+ days post-vaccination, 3+ months before entry); no mandatory quarantine if documentation complete
    Morocco is not on the rabies-free/low-risk list, so the full import path applies — a real multi-month (3-4mo) lead time, workable but not a footnote
  • Private health insurance — monthly cost
    50–200 $/month
    $50-150/month local Moroccan plans; $100-200/month international-coverage plans. No age-tiered rate card found from any source.
  • Private health insurance — new-applicant age enrollment ceiling
    ~65-70 (insurer-dependent); 'extremely expensive' past 60 years
    Not a formal visa-eligibility gate — the Inactif route's own documentation list requires a medical certificate but not proof of insurance, unlike the Student visa's explicit €30,000 minimum-coverage requirement. A real practical cost/access risk for an older applicant, single-sourced for the exact ages.
  • Property ownership restricted-zone equivalent
    No
    Full freehold nationwide, no coastal/border band equivalent to Guatemala/Mexico — a genuine structural difference, cross-checked independently
  • Public (AMO/RAMED) health insurance access for non-employed foreign residents
    No
    Formal employment or permanent residency triggers enrollment in the public health-insurance system (AMO); a Carte de Séjour pour Inactif holder is not automatically covered and must arrange private insurance. Public ER departments remain accessible to all in a genuine emergency regardless of coverage.
  • Regime shape
    open-freehold
    Full freehold nationwide, no coastal/border band equivalent to Guatemala/Mexico.
  • Tax residency worldwide-income trigger
    183 days/year
    Not territorial like Guatemala's system
  • Tenure mechanism — direct freehold
    Yes
    Agricultural land is a real exception, not covered by this mechanism.
  • Tenure mechanism legality — direct freehold
    legitimate
  • Restricted-nationality list (e-Visa required instead of 90-day visa-free entry)
    BJ, BN, GT, IL, IN, JO, KH, LA, TH, VN
    Benin, Brunei, Cambodia, Guatemala, India, Israel, Jordan, Laos, Thailand, and Vietnam are routed onto Morocco's e-Visa track (30 days, one 30-day extension, 60 days total) instead of the standard 90-day visa-free tier (one 90-day extension, 180 days total) most Western passports get. Source list explicitly non-exhaustive ('among others') per moroccan-visa.com/e-visamorocco.com/Wikipedia's Visa policy of Morocco (cross-corroborated 2026-07-08) - treat these 10 codes as a confirmed floor, not a confirmed-complete set. A narrower carve-out exists for Israeli citizens born in Morocco (visa on arrival with Moroccan national ID) or holding a Council of Jewish Communities of Morocco letter (evis.ma) - not reflected in this row, which states the general-population rule.

Community

Who else lives here, how you'd actually meet them, and what it's like once the novelty wears off.

  • Organized anti-nomad backlash
    No
    Locals' own reaction described as genuinely mixed, not uniformly negative — 'some locals have valid worries about gentrification and rapid change, [but] many see this growth as a positive step' (Middle East Eye) — no organized anti-tourist/anti-nomad protest or campaign found.
  • Fishing-village house growth (quantified structural shift)
    200 -> ~800 houses
    Village grew from 200 original fishing-family houses to roughly four times as many (~800), with only 60-70 fishing boats now remaining — a real, quantified generational shift away from the town's original economic base.
  • Group/compound viability — untitled-land diligence multiplies per parcel (village/Tamraght corridor)
    Yes
    Correction (2026-07-08): the same untitled-village-land risk (>95% of village transactions done without processed titles) applies per parcel in the village/Tamraght/Imi Ouaddar corridor — the prior claim that newer titre-foncier stock reduces a group's diligence burden here was wrong and has been corrected; a group assembling adjacent parcels in this corridor faces the same multiplying-diligence-burden logic as Marrakech's medina, just for a differently-shaped title risk.
  • Group/compound viability — real-estate stock shape (newer construction, not family riads)
    Villas, apartments, buildable plots (Tamraght-Imi Ouaddar corridor); Taghazout Bay formal state-ceded development also present
    Taghazout's stock is largely newer construction rather than Marrakech's generations-old medina riads — but this sits inside a rapidly growing, government-driven tourism development (Taghazout Bay) with a real, named environmental-sustainability question mark (golf-course water use) that a group settling permanently would be building into, not around. Not evaluated against a real group scenario this pass.
Red flags

The hard truths, stated plainly — real risks, sitting right next to everything that's actually going well.

  • Homicide rate
    1.2 per 100,000
    Comparable to Portugal (0.8), well below the global average
  • LGBTQ+ criminalization (Art. 489 Penal Code)
    Yes
    3-5 years imprisonment; domestic law since 1962; enforcement sporadic but real, names publicized before trial
  • Political trajectory (GenZ 212 protests, succession speculation)
    active, unresolved
    Largest protest wave since 2011, Sept-Oct 2025; September 2026 general elections outcome open
  • Structural water stress
    ~620 m³/person/year
    Close to absolute-scarcity threshold; masked short-term by one good rainy season (Jan 2026 rainfall +95% YoY) — two tracks pointing opposite directions, both real
  • Political Stability Index (World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators)
    -0.44 index (-2.5 weak to +2.5 strong; 2024 figure, up from -0.53 in 2023)
    TradingEconomics/World Bank WGI; sits somewhat below the world average of -0.07 — a genuinely middling, slightly-improving baseline, not an outlier-low score
  • Dirham currency-peg transition (mid-float, 2026-2027 target)
    Bank Al-Maghrib widened the trading band from 0.3% to 2.5% (2018) then 5% (2020, still pegged to a 60% euro/40% dollar basket); 2026 pilot phase for inflation-targeting, full float/effective implementation targeted late 2026/early 2027
    FXTradingAfrica, DabaFinance, Bloomberg, MoroccoWorldNews, citing Governor Abdellatif Jouahri directly. Over 80% of Moroccan companies reported as too small to be prepared for real exchange-rate risk — the central bank's own stated reason for moving cautiously rather than on the IMF's preferred faster timeline. A real, deliberate, gradual transition to watch, not a currency crisis or abrupt devaluation risk
  • Foreign-investment/capital-controls posture — IGOC 2026 reform
    Instruction Générale des Opérations de Change (IGOC) 2026, effective Jan 1 2026 — the most significant reform to Morocco's capital-controls regime in years; resident foreign investors holding investments 10+ years can transfer up to MAD 2M/year (~€185,000) abroad without original proof-of-financing documents; individual travel/transfer allowances raised across the board
    Primary source: the Office des Changes (Morocco's own foreign-exchange regulatory authority) — its own publication announcing IGOC 2026, corroborated by Lawyard, AMERELLER, MoroccoWorldNews, NorthAfricaPost. Sits on top of the 2022 Investment Charter, still in active rollout. No tightening, expropriation threat, or foreign-ownership rule reversal found anywhere this pass — the confirmed direction of travel for foreign capital specifically is opening, not closing
  • Earthquake/tsunami exposure (1960 Agadir / 1755 Lisbon)
    Yes
    1960 quake killed ~10,000-15,000, ~1/3 of Agadir's population; the single largest-scale historical hazard found for any Morocco location, larger even than Marrakech's own
  • Internet reliability
    weakest of Morocco's 5 nomad hubs
    Coworking venues' own 200 Mbps fiber solves the problem only while physically there — a real, structural, more-than-inconvenience risk to remote-work income viability
  • Taghazout Bay golf-course water use
    Yes
    Reads directly against Morocco's national structural water-stress finding — not yet organized backlash, a real sustainability question mark
