  • Carte de Séjour pour Inactif documentation shape
    A Moroccan bank certificate (attestation bancaire) showing sufficient funds, 3-6 months of bank statements, an apostilled clean criminal-record check, a Moroccan medical certificate, and proof of accommodation (lease or title deed). Notably absent: no health-insurance requirement at all — unlike Morocco's student category, which demands a €30,000 medical/repatriation policy. The honest open edge is WHICH financial proof shape a given prefecture actually tests: the sourcing carries both a recurring monthly-income framing (the $1,000-1,650/month cluster) and a one-time lump-sum bank-balance framing (~100,000 MAD shown at application, withdrawable after), with renewal practice reported to test documentation quality hard — cash-only applicants are often refused; clear bank records are what pass.
  • Carte de Séjour pour Inactif — nationality restriction
    No
  • Employer-sponsored work visa — ANAPEC certificate exemption for Algeria/Tunisia/Senegal nationals
    Exempt from the ANAPEC non-availability-certificate requirement (not exempt from the route itself): employees seconded for limited periods by a foreign company; cooperation-program delegates for stays under 6 months; and nationals of countries holding a bilateral establishment/employment agreement with Morocco — Algeria, Tunisia, and Senegal named specifically.
  • Carte de Sejour pour Inactif -- max duration (initial validity)
    1 years
  • Visa-free entry, most nationalities -- max duration
    90 days
  • Visa-free entry, most nationalities -- renewability (extension)
    renewable-limited: one extension of up to another 90 days, 180-day maximum total on the visa-exempt track (via the Bureau des Etrangers; discretionary, not automatic)
  • Remote work on tourist/inactive status — gray-area tolerance and employment-income tax incentive
    Working remotely for a foreign client/employer while on tourist or inactive-resident status is tolerated in practice (foreign-sourced, foreign-paid income), not an explicit legal right; Moroccan-sourced income earned on tourist status is a clear violation. Tax residents (183+ days/year) are taxed on worldwide income. A 20% flat tax rate (Article 10, Finance Law) applies to certain digital-nomad/expatriate employment income from non-resident employers, but freelance/passive income is reported to fall under a different regime ('Professional Income') and this pass could not confirm how a passive-income source would be classified.

Red flags

The hard truths, stated plainly — real risks, sitting right next to everything that's actually going well.

  • Structural water stress
    ~620 m³/person/year
  • Political Stability Index (World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators)
    -0.44 index (-2.5 weak to +2.5 strong; 2024 figure, up from -0.53 in 2023)
  • Dirham currency-peg transition (mid-float, 2026-2027 target)
    Bank Al-Maghrib widened the trading band from 0.3% to 2.5% (2018) then 5% (2020, still pegged to a 60% euro/40% dollar basket); 2026 pilot phase for inflation-targeting, full float/effective implementation targeted late 2026/early 2027
  • Foreign-investment/capital-controls posture — IGOC 2026 reform
    Instruction Générale des Opérations de Change (IGOC) 2026, effective Jan 1 2026 — the most significant reform to Morocco's capital-controls regime in years; resident foreign investors holding investments 10+ years can transfer up to MAD 2M/year (~€185,000) abroad without original proof-of-financing documents; individual travel/transfer allowances raised across the board
  • Earthquake/tsunami exposure (1960 Agadir / 1755 Lisbon)
    Yes
  • Internet reliability
    Residential/ADSL wifi widely unreliable for video calls outside two named coworking spaces (SunDesk, AdventureKeys), each separately advertising ~200 Mbps fiber on their own sites
  • Taghazout Bay golf-course water use
    Yes
  • Weak local internet — structural income-viability risk
    ADSL-only in most apartments/hotels outside two named coworking spaces (SunDesk, AdventureKeys), each separately advertising ~200 Mbps fiber on their own sites; basic ADSL connections handle email/browsing but struggle with video calls especially during peak hours; mobile data backup recommended
