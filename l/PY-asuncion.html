Overview
  • Territorial tax system (foreign-sourced income exemption)
    Permanent, no time limit — remote work, dividends, rental, capital gains, pensions all exempt
  • Paraguay-source income test — physical work location, not client/payer location (Law 6380/2019 Art. 6)
    Paraguay's territorial IRP system draws its source-of-income line at WHERE THE ACTIVITY IS PHYSICALLY PERFORMED, not who pays or where a service is 'delivered.' Law 6380/2019 Art. 6's own text (confirmed directly against DNIT's official portal copy): 'Constituiran rentas de fuente paraguaya las que provienen de actividades desarrolladas en la Republica, de bienes situados o de derechos utilizados economicamente en la misma.' Genuinely foreign-sourced income (foreign dividends, foreign pensions, foreign capital gains, and work physically performed outside Paraguay) sits outside IRP's scope entirely. But a remote worker or consultant physically living and working from a desk in Asuncion for a foreign client is, on this test, generating Paraguay-source personal-services income -- taxed on the progressive 8-10% scale, itself exempt below roughly PYG 80 million/year (~$12,000) -- regardless of the client's nationality, location, or currency. Paraguay-sourced capital income is a flat 8%.
  • Climate — hot, humid, seasonal extremes
    18.7°C (July avg) to 28.8°C (January avg), highs to 38-40°C
  • Nature & water-adjacency — Costanera de Asunción
    ~15km riverside park/promenade on the Paraguay River
  • No primary Paraguayan institutional source directly fetched
    Partially resolved 2026-07-18 — real primary URLs found and cited (migraciones.gov.py, mic.gov.py, suace.gov.py, transparency.org), but migraciones.gov.py/mic.gov.py themselves returned DNS/connection errors on a direct fetch this session — genuinely found, not yet genuinely read
  • Pace of life — relationship-over-efficiency daily rhythm
    Daily rhythm organized around relationship and presence rather than schedules — mornings begin with tereré (cold yerba mate), afternoons outdoors, evenings with family/neighbors rather than screens
  • Pace of life — bureaucratic slowness, named as a real friction not just a cultural-adjustment reframe
    Administrative processes (bank accounts, official documents) described as archaic: stamps, paper forms across multiple counters, limited opening hours, requiring repeat visits
  • Pace of life — late-shifted daily/evening rhythm
    Dinner commonly 9-10pm, bars after 11pm — a late-shifted daily rhythm relative to North American/Northern European norms
  • Pace of life — outdoor-oriented daily life enabled by climate
    "Most of life happens outdoors — close to nature, and always moving"; abundant undeveloped/informal outdoor access described as free of crowds, entry fees, or posted restrictions
  • Nature & water-adjacency — Jardín Botánico y Zoológico
    A city-center botanical garden and zoo, described as making Asunción 'feel more arboreal than most capitals' — a second real green-space asset distinct from the Costanera
  • Nature & water-adjacency — landlocked, no coastal/beach access
    Paraguay is entirely landlocked — "no idyllic beaches on the coast," a structural (not research-gap) limit on water recreation relative to other coastal candidates on this site

  • Bank account access for foreigners — gated by residency, not by income type
    Paraguayan banks treat non-resident foreigners on a tourist entry or temporary migration card differently from residents -- the source's own language is that 'non-residents may face limitations,' not that account opening is impossible or categorically very hard (no source found this pass supports that stronger wording). Once a foreigner holds an actual residency permit and the resulting cedula (national ID), account opening is straightforward, and foreign-sourced income of any of the qualifying types (corporate/personal bank statements, brokerage/investment account summaries, rental agreements or contracts, audited financial reports, dividend certificates, tax filings or returns) is accepted as proof of funds without requiring local employment. With a cedula, both guarani and US-dollar accounts can be opened, and international SWIFT transfers are received with no exchange controls.

Community

Who else lives here, how you'd actually meet them, and what it's like once the novelty wears off.

  • Established foreign-resident population
    47,687 residency applications submitted to Paraguay nationally in 2025 (63% YoY increase), 40,600 granted (42% YoY increase) — Brazil 23,526 (58%), Argentina 4,366, Germany 1,652, Bolivia 1,357, Spain 1,023, Venezuela 847, Netherlands 772, US 736, Russia 509, France 492. No Asunción-specific headcount found; Asunción and Encarnación named as the two main expat hubs
  • Gentrification / gringo-pricing
    Real, named, but thinner evidence than Cuenca's: a first-hand account directly advises disguising a foreign accent so 'landlords won't catch on... and try to charge you more'; a separate rental bait-and-switch pattern on Facebook Marketplace; rents rose 6-8% (2023) and 10-12% (2025), attributed partly to expat/digital-nomad/business-traveler demand. No documented specific local-displacement case (à la Cuenca's pharmacy/rental-ad pair) was found
  • Room for others — documented ecovillage attempt cancelled
    A permaculture-village project sought like-minded people, cancelled outright from insufficient interest (down to 2 people)
  • Room for others / group viability — raw rural land price
    50–100 $/hectare
  • Subjective community read — small/developing scene
    'Still developing,' no massive co-living spaces, limited expat events, few nomad services vs. Bali/Mexico
Red flags

The hard truths, stated plainly — real risks, sitting right next to everything that's actually going well.

  • Cannabis law
    Personal use ≤10g decriminalized under Article 30 of Law 1340, but judged case-by-case; cultivation 10-20 years
  • Corruption Perceptions Index score
    24 /100 (150th of 182 countries)
  • Democratic stabilization since 1989 (fall of Stroessner dictatorship)
    Real institutional progress, but 'rule of law remains weak, little progress fighting corruption'
  • Dengue fever (endemic)
    Yes, year-round, peaking Oct-Apr
  • Semi-public healthcare access via the Social Security Institute (IPS) — employment-contribution gate
    Employment-linked; requires proof of employment plus employer/employee payroll contributions
  • Kidnapping/cartel-extortion risk specifically targeting foreigners
    Two distinct threats: a Brazilian prison-gang mafia (PCC), trafficking-led, concentrated in Ciudad del Este/tri-border/Pedro Juan Caballero, no documented foreigner-specific targeting found; a small Marxist-Leninist guerrilla group (the EPP, ~50-100 fighters), a real, dated, confirmed pattern of kidnapping-for-ransom targeting foreign-born/foreign-descended ranchers (Mennonites, Germans), but geographically confined to Concepción/San Pedro/Amambay departments, ~300-450km from Asunción, with no foreigner-specific case found post-2017
  • Law 7389/24 — National Regime for Integrity, Transparency and Prevention of Corruption
    Yes (enacted 2024)
  • Legal drinking age
    20 (most authoritative sources); one lower-authority source claims 18 — unresolved years
  • National homicide rate
    6.2 per 100,000
  • Pet import (dog and cat) — rabies titer / quarantine requirement
    No rabies titer required for entry; vaccination 30 days–12 months prior; Spanish-translated health certificate within 10 days
  • Petty crime pattern (motochorros)
    Yes
  • Police corruption at traffic stops
    Yes
  • Private health insurance — international-insurer annual premium (couple, age 60-70)
    4000–8000 $/year
  • Private health insurance — international-insurer new-enrollment age cap
    75 years
  • Private health insurance — local-insurer new-enrollment age cap
    65, or 65–70 depending on the plan years
  • Private health insurance — local monthly premium
    30–100 $/month
  • Public healthcare (MSPBS) access for foreigners
    Free to anyone with valid ID, regardless of residency status
  • Rentista/Pensioner visa — health insurance proof requirement
    Genuinely conflicting across sources — recommended-only per one source, required-for-proof-of-means per another
  • Road safety (traffic deaths)
    22.6 per 100,000
  • The Cartes factor — ongoing informal-influence question
    US lifted sanctions on former president Cartes Oct 2025; informal influence described as continuing, contributing to legislative gridlock
  • Distance to emergency care
    Public-hospital ER quality unreliable; private facilities (La Costa, San Roque, Bautista, Hospital del Trauma) preferred
  • Doctor/nurse staffing ratio (national)
    11.1 doctors, 17.9 nurses per 10,000 population
  • First-hand account of navigating the healthcare system as a foreign resident
    A foreign resident of 'several years' (as of Feb 2025 publication) describes a public-hospital visit ('paint peeling off the walls... chaotic waiting room') alongside a free yellow-fever vaccine obtained at a public health building; her regular private-care costs run ~$35 (dental cleaning), ~$25 (dermatologist consult), ~$200 (24-test private-clinic bloodwork), $30-100/month (private insurance) — managed almost entirely via WhatsApp
  • Internet speed (independently measured)
    174.2 dl / 135.2 ul Mbps
  • Medevac line — where local capability ends
    For genuinely severe conditions, many expats fly to Brazil or Argentina; medevac coverage described as 'not optional'
  • Named areas to avoid at night
    Chacarita (named as deserving special caution), plus poorly-lit riverfront edges, isolated bus stops, and areas around cash machines after dark
  • Private hospital cost — cardiac surgery (local-complex-care proxy)
    8000–25000 $
  • Private hospital cost — GP / specialist / hospital day rate
    GP $25-50, specialist $30-80, hospital day rate $80-200 $ (per visit/day)
