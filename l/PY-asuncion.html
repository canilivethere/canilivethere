  • Territorial tax system (foreign-sourced income exemption)
    Permanent, no time limit — remote work, dividends, rental, capital gains, pensions all exempt
    Unlike Uruguay's 10-year tax holiday, described as permanent — a real structural draw. source URL is domain-level best-effort (2026-07-18), not re-confirmed
  • Paraguay-source income test — physical work location, not client/payer location (Law 6380/2019 Art. 6)
    Paraguay's territorial IRP system draws its source-of-income line at WHERE THE ACTIVITY IS PHYSICALLY PERFORMED, not who pays or where a service is 'delivered.' Law 6380/2019 Art. 6's own text (confirmed directly against DNIT's official portal copy): 'Constituiran rentas de fuente paraguaya las que provienen de actividades desarrolladas en la Republica, de bienes situados o de derechos utilizados economicamente en la misma.' Genuinely foreign-sourced income (foreign dividends, foreign pensions, foreign capital gains, and work physically performed outside Paraguay) sits outside IRP's scope entirely. But a remote worker or consultant physically living and working from a desk in Asuncion for a foreign client is, on this test, generating Paraguay-source personal-services income -- taxed on the progressive 8-10% scale, itself exempt below roughly PYG 80 million/year (~$12,000) -- regardless of the client's nationality, location, or currency. Paraguay-sourced capital income is a flat 8%.

Re-checked directly: fetched Art. 6's text from DNIT's own official portal (dnit.gov.py, Paraguay's tax authority -- primary-institutional tier, not aggregator paraphrase) and independently confirmed the physical-performance test both there and by re-reading the original cited source (paraguaysovereign.com/tax/territorial-tax-explained/), which itself states this plainly for exactly this reader: work 'performed from Asuncion may... be treated as local-source regardless of where the employer is located,' and 'do not assume 0% without professional advice' -- both points genuinely present in that source but omitted from the prior version of this row. Practical upshot for the headline 0% claim: it mainly protects passive/foreign-situs income (foreign dividends, pensions, capital gains) and work actually performed while physically outside Paraguay -- NOT the day-to-day remote-work case of someone sitting in Asuncion billing a foreign client, which this test places inside Paraguay-source scope.
