  • Foreign-sourced income taxation on remittance — 2024 rule, 2026 proposed softening
    Since 2024, a Thai tax resident (180+ days/year) who remits foreign-sourced income to Thailand in the year it is earned owes Thai personal income tax on it, regardless of visa type (except LTR's statutory exemption above). A Revenue Department proposal, not yet formally enacted as of this research, would exempt foreign-sourced income from tax if remitted within the year earned or the following year, intended to apply from the 2026 tax year onward.

  • Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) documentation shape
    A bank balance of 500,000 THB held for at least 3 months before applying — cryptocurrency and investment-portfolio balances are explicitly not accepted — plus a clean immigration record, and the application must be filed from outside Thailand on the official e-Visa platform (which reportedly checks IP address and GPS data to enforce that rule). The remote-worker (Workcation) category then demands proof of ACTIVE work: an employment contract or employer letter explicitly authorizing remote work, or client contracts/invoices showing an ongoing income pattern over roughly 3-6 months, or business registration with revenue proof. Passive income alone (investment, dividend, savings-drawdown) is explicitly described as insufficient as primary proof — it can supplement active-income documentation but cannot substitute for it.
  • O-A / O-X documentation shape
    One documentation row covering both tiers of this route. O-A (1-year): 800,000 THB held in a Thai bank account for at least 2 months before application (and not dropping below 400,000 THB during the visa year), OR a documented monthly income of at least 65,000 THB (typically evidenced by an embassy income affidavit), OR a combination of deposit plus annual income totaling 800,000 THB — plus mandatory health insurance from an insurer approved by Thailand's insurance regulator (OIC), minimum 40,000 THB outpatient / 400,000 THB inpatient annual coverage. O-X (5-10 year): a security deposit of at least 3,000,000 THB in a Thai bank. Ordinary pension/retirement/investment income is the accepted evidence class — no active-employment documentation standard exists on this route.
  • O-A documentation shape
    800,000 THB held in a Thai bank account for at least 2 months before application (balance can't drop below 400,000 THB during the visa year), OR a documented monthly income of at least 65,000 THB (typically evidenced by an embassy income affidavit), OR a combination of deposit plus annual income totaling 800,000 THB — plus mandatory health insurance from an insurer approved by Thailand's insurance regulator (OIC), minimum 40,000 THB outpatient / 400,000 THB inpatient annual coverage. Ordinary pension/retirement/investment income is the accepted evidence class — no active-employment documentation standard exists on this route.
  • O-X documentation shape
    A security deposit of at least 3,000,000 THB held in a Thai bank account. Ordinary pension/retirement/investment income is the accepted evidence class — no active-employment documentation standard exists on this route. This tier additionally carries its own 14-country eligible-nationality list, gated separately — see TH:o-x-nationality-restriction.
