Visa & residency

How you'd actually get to stay — the real routes, their income floors, and how long they realistically take, not the marketing version.

  • Remote work for a foreign employer/clients has no legal visa or work-permit category
    No official authorization exists — "No visa or permit category covers ordinary remote work for overseas clients"
    New fact, 2026-07-18 (Linh, thinnest-3-criteria wave) — Income viability carried zero fact rows before this pass, a genuine gap now closed with the single most load-bearing finding in it. Vietnam's Labor Code (Law 45/2019/QH14), Article 151, requires foreign nationals "working in Vietnam" to hold a valid work permit unless an Article 154 exemption applies; the source states plainly no e-visa work category exists and "officers can ask about your activity." Divergence flag set Confirmed-diverges (not Not-yet-checked) because the same source explicitly documents the paper-vs-practice gap itself, not just the legal mechanism: "In practice, many long-stay remote workers use the 90-day e-visa, work online for employers and clients outside Vietnam, and leave to reset when it expires" — but "None of this is legal certainty... the rules have tightened before." Working without a required permit carries a fine of VND 15-25 million plus expulsion
  • Working without a required work permit — penalty
    15000000–25000000 VND fine + expulsion
    New fact, 2026-07-18 (Linh). ~$590-980 USD at ~25,500 VND/USD, plus expulsion from the country — the expulsion consequence is the more structurally significant half of this penalty, not the fine amount itself
  • Personal income tax — tax-residency threshold (183-day rule)
    183 days (aggregate, within one calendar year or a consecutive 12-month period)
    New fact, 2026-07-18 (Linh). Source is a 2022-dated secondary legal-guide summary of standing Vietnamese PIT law (Law on Personal Income Tax, amended 2012/2014) — the underlying 183-day rule and rate structure are longstanding, not a recent change, but this was not independently re-verified against a 2026 primary Vietnamese tax-authority (General Department of Taxation) source this pass, the same primary-institutional-source gap already flagged project-wide for Vietnam. Medium confidence on continued 2026 accuracy of the exact figures, not Low — this is standing structural tax law, not a fast-moving policy. Staying 90-183 days may still be treated as non-resident if tax residency elsewhere is proven
  • Personal income tax — resident progressive rates
    5–35 % (progressive brackets, on worldwide income)
    New fact, 2026-07-18 (Linh). Once tax-resident (183+ days), the Law on Personal Income Tax covers "the worldwide income of Vietnam residents" — a real, structural finding directly relevant to anyone weighing whether staying under 183 days per rolling period could avoid Vietnamese tax on foreign-sourced remote income; specific bracket thresholds (the VND income levels each rate applies to) were not detailed in this pass's own source text — a named gap
  • Personal income tax — non-resident flat rate
    20 % flat (on Vietnam-sourced income only)
    New fact, 2026-07-18 (Linh). Non-residents (under 183 days, no permanent home) are taxed only on Vietnam-sourced income, not worldwide income — meaning a non-resident's genuinely foreign-sourced remote income sits outside Vietnamese PIT's reach on paper, though a 2026-07-01 source (see the remote-work-legality row) notes "the work is physically performed in Vietnam," so the sourcing characterization of that specific income "still needs advice" — a real, live ambiguity, not a clean answer either way
  • Any Temporary Residence Card (TRC) route converts to Permanent Residence / naturalization
    Not yet researched
    Vietnam has a PR-card mechanism in principle, but no clear, sourced eligibility timeline or in-practice process description was found this pass
  • Border-run tolerance
    Normal, current, tolerated — no active crackdown, cumulative-day cap, or pattern-based enforcement found
    A real, meaningful contrast with Thailand's 2025-2026 tightening — but explicitly named as current-as-of-this-pass, not permanent (border-run tolerance can shift with little notice, per Thailand's own precedent)
  • e-Visa extension possibility
    Not yet researched
    One source states a one-time 90-day extension is officially available; most other sources say extension beyond 90 days is generally not available and exit-reapply is standard practice instead
  • e-Visa stay allowance
    90 days
    Applies to all countries/territories as of 2026; single- or multi-entry; USD 25 fee. Longer than Thailand's post-rollback 30-day exemption, shorter than Malaysia's fee-free 90-day exemption
  • Foreign-ownership quota — apartment building
    30 % of units per building
    Once reached, no further units in that building can be sold to foreigners — the same structural shape as Thailand's 49% condo cap, just a lower number
  • Foreign-ownership quota — houses per ward-equivalent unit
    250 houses (incl. villas/townhouses)
    A second, separate cap layered on top of the 30% building-unit quota
  • Land/property access — paper-vs-practice divergence state
    Not yet checked
    Same `HardConstraintRule`-shaped mechanism category as Thailand's condo-quota system; no lived account of the quota system, renewal process, or resale-inheritance mechanic found this pass
  • No retirement, passive-income, or digital-nomad visa exists
    Yes (confirmed absent)
    Long-term legal stay rests entirely on a Temporary Residence Card (TRC) tied to a qualifying purpose (employment, investment, marriage/family, sponsored org work) — confirmed directly, not just inherited from an earlier scan
  • Overstay penalty schedule
    Not yet researched
    General understanding is fines/deportation/blacklisting apply, but no specific fine schedule or ban-duration ladder confirmed this pass
  • Regime shape
    closed-except-single-vehicle
    Flagged: no primary statute number found in this project's Vietnam research this pass — the composite-name fallback per §4's own grammar. A property.md doesn't exist for Vietnam yet either.
  • Resale-unit lease-term inheritance
    Resale buyer inherits the remaining lease term, not a fresh 50 years — clock starts at original certificate issuance
    A real, easy-to-miss detail for anyone considering a resale purchase rather than new-build
  • Tenure mechanism — direct freehold (land)
    No
    Genuinely different from both India's near-total bar and Guatemala's/Mexico's freehold models — a defined, if term-limited, purchasable interest exists here. No mechanism_legality companion row added — this is a structural absence of a freehold option, not a punished workaround.
  • Tenure mechanism — leasehold (Land Use Right)
    Yes (50yr, renewable once to 100yr total) years
    Not a freehold purchase of land — buying a condo means buying the right to occupy the unit for the lease period. Two separate quotas layered on top: 30% of units per building, 250 houses per ward-equivalent unit. Resale buyer inherits the remaining lease term, not a fresh 50 years.

    legitimate
  • Temporary Residence Card (TRC) via Employment — structure
    Requires a Vietnamese work permit; a Temporary Residence Card (TRC) is then issued for 6 months to 5 years depending on underlying category
    Not applicable without a genuine qualifying Vietnamese (or foreign-invested Vietnam-based) employer
  • Temporary Residence Card (TRC) via Employment accepts passive income as qualifying proof
    No
    Active employment only — no passive-income equivalent
  • Temporary Residence Card (TRC) via Marriage/Family — precondition-gated
    Real but conditional — requires a Vietnamese-citizen spouse/family member
    Not independently researched to the mechanical depth of Thailand's marriage-visa section (financial thresholds, renewal cadence, PR-equivalent eligibility) — a named gap
  • Temporary Residence Card (TRC) via Registered Investment — capital threshold
    Not yet researched
    A qualifying registered investment (e.g. establishing/investing in a Vietnamese company) qualifies, but no consistent minimum-capital figure found, unlike Thailand's clearly tiered ฿3M/฿10M/$1M structure

Red flags

The hard truths, stated plainly — real risks, sitting right next to everything that's actually going well.

  • 2026 leadership consolidation (Tô Lâm)
    General Secretary + President combined in one person for the first time in 4 decades; renewed 5-year mandate, retirement-age exemption
    New Regulation 196 (June 2026) further strengthens party-committee authority inside state-owned firms
  • Cannabis law
    Illegal, death penalty for trafficking 5kg+ resin / 75kg+ leaves-stems-seeds (Art. 251); 2-7yr imprisonment for simple possession 1-500g (Art. 249)
    Materially harsher than Goa's cannabis regime — applies to foreigners exactly as to Vietnamese nationals, no exception
  • Foreign investment posture
    A real, current draw — 2024 US FDI stock $4.4B, up 8% from 2023, driven by China-cost/tariff-driven supply-chain diversification
    Named frictions remain real: high tariffs on food/agricultural products, limited regulatory transparency, inadequate IP protection
  • One-party political system (CPV)
    Durably controlled; genuine political stability expected precisely because dissent is suppressed and power is centralized
    Not a fragile-coalition kind of stability the way several other candidates show — worth naming both the stability and the authoritarian governance model plainly
  • Pet import (dog and cat) — rabies titer / quarantine requirement

    A fourth exception to this project's dog-only pet-import gap (both species researched together).
