  • Foreign national public-healthcare eligibility
    Legally resident foreigners (valid visa/permit) constitutionally entitled to public healthcare on the same income-sliding-scale subsidy as citizens (Constitution Section 27; National Health Act Section 4(3)); tourists NOT eligible for free public care; emergency care available to anyone regardless of status, including undocumented persons
    An income-qualifying visa holder (Retired Person's/Remote Work Visa tier) sits above the subsidized bracket and would pay close to full price even inside the public system — legally open, not functionally the real door for this income profile. Upgraded 2026-07-18: primary source is now the South African Human Rights Commission's (SAHRC — a constitutionally-established statutory body) own media advisory, naming the exact legal mechanism (Constitution Section 27, National Health Act Section 4(3)); permanent residents get citizen-equivalent rights, temporary residents get sliding-scale means-tested pricing, undocumented persons keep an unconditional constitutional right to emergency care specifically. divergence_flag set to Confirmed-matches on the strength of this direct statutory citation, not merely aggregator paraphrase

  • Medical aid scheme age-discrimination ban
    No confirmed maximum enrollment age — Medical Schemes Act 1998 bars age-based acceptance discrimination; late-joiner premium surcharge applies to anyone first joining a scheme at age 35+ (or after a 3+ month coverage break): +5% (1-4 uncovered years after 35), +25% (5-14 years), +50% (15-24 years), +75% (25+ years) — permanent for the membership's duration, applied to the base contribution
    A genuine positive divergence from the 65-75 private-insurance enrollment-ceiling pattern found in several other candidate countries — no ceiling found here, and the penalty structure now quantified precisely (four tiers) rather than described vaguely. Exempted: members continuously covered since before 1 April 2001.     Directly relevant to the redefined "Income viability" criterion (per [on file]: not scoped to remote/passive income only — ceramics, a café, in-person tutoring are live possibilities too) — this is the actual compliance mechanism anyone earning non-salary income inside South Africa's tax-residency net would face, not previously captured as a fact row. Visa & residency

How you'd actually get to stay — the real routes, their income floors, and how long they realistically take, not the marketing version.

  • Border-run practice/tolerance
    Not yet researched
    Genuinely unresolved, not a confirmed "common and tolerated" or "under crackdown" read either way
  • Business Visa — capital requirement
    275000 USD (ZAR 5M, unencumbered)
    Requires a chartered-accountant-endorsed business plan + commitment to employ 60%+ SA citizens/PRs; capital waiver possible in nationally-prioritized sectors (ICT, textiles, chemicals/biotech, agro-processing, metals/minerals, automotive) at the discretion of the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (DTIC). Primary source: South African Embassy's own requirements PDF, cross-corroborated with citizenremote.com and ibn.co.za
  • Critical Skills Work Visa — precondition-gated
    Requires a profession on South Africa's published Critical Skills List; a 2024 points-based system now also scores qualifications, salary, experience, language skills, and Trusted Employer status (100 automatic points for a listed occupation)
    Not tied to a single employer; 5 years continuous residence/employment in the critical skill can qualify for PR. Issued for up to 5 years; minimum processing time 20 days. Primary source: South Africa's Department of Home Affairs (DHA)'s own requirements PDF, directly fetched 2026-07-18, cross-corroborated with fragomen.com and xpatweb.com's coverage of the same 2024 points-based system
  • Diligence risk — 2024 Expropriation Act, without-compensation provisions
    Yes
    Signed into law Jan 2025, but not yet in force as of this pass. The 2026-07-11 pass's "no source found states whether this applies differently to foreign-owned vs. citizen-owned property" is now resolved: privateproperty.co.za states plainly "foreign investors are not exempt" — if government determines a foreign-owned property is needed for public projects or land reform, it can be taken under the same conditions as local property owners, hence divergence_flag upgraded from "Not yet checked" to "Confirmed-matches" (foreign owners face the same treatment as citizens, not worse — but also not better). The Ekurhuleni test case (34-hectare Farm Driefontein property, valued R30-64M, expropriated without compensation in 2019, still unresolved) completed court-directed mediation October 2025 and now has an 18-day trial scheduled for February 2026 — cross-corroborated across sakeliga.org.za, ewn.co.za, dailyinvestor.com, gauteng.news, and moneyweb.co.za, all independently confirming the same trial date
  • Family/Spousal and Life Partner Visa — precondition-gated
    Requires an existing SA-citizen/PR spouse, or a 2+-year exclusivity/cohabitation/mutual-support life partnership under Section 11(6) of the Immigration Act
    The sponsor must show proof of financial means to support the foreign spouse to a minimum of R8,500/month (updated 2026-07-18 — a more specific and slightly different figure than the previously-cited ZAR 3,000/month; the R8,500 figure is the one confirmed this pass across multiple sources including workpermitsouthafrica.co.za's own information sheet, not yet reconciled against the earlier ZAR 3,000 claim, which may reflect an older or different sub-category). Life Partner route is genuinely more accessible on paper than Belize's marriage-only equivalent (doesn't require an actual marriage) — same-sex life partnerships confirmed to carry identical rights. Standard processing 8-10 weeks in-country, 4-10 weeks at a mission abroad. PR available once the relationship exceeds 5 years
  • Financial Independence Permit — net-worth requirement
    650000 USD (ZAR 12M, global net worth)
    Plus a once-off ZAR 120,000 fee; grants immediate Permanent Residence, not temporary/renewable status
  • Overstay penalty
    Declared 'undesirable person': 12 months' re-entry ban for an overstay of 30 days or less; 5 years' ban for an overstay exceeding 30 days, plus potential fines
    Upgraded 2026-07-18 from a single-source, imprecise "1-5 years" range to the exact two-tier structure, cross-corroborated with xpatweb.com's identical undesirable-status description. An extension application already filed and pending protects against an undesirable declaration while under review; a 10-day appeal window exists once declared
  • Property ownership mechanism (foreigners)
    No general foreign-ownership ban, no city-level foreigner quota, no foreigner-only property tax
    Foreigners purchase with essentially the same rights as citizens — closer to Belize's full-freehold model than to Mexico's/Guatemala's restricted-coastal-zone fideicomiso structures. divergence_flag upgraded 2026-07-18 alongside the Expropriation Act row above — the same direct source confirms foreign owners face identical (not worse, not better) treatment under the open regime and under the Act's own without-compensation provisions alike
  • Regime shape
    open-freehold
    No general foreign-ownership ban, no city-level foreigner quota, no foreigner-only property tax — closer to Belize's full-freehold model than Mexico's/Guatemala's restricted-coastal-zone structures.
  • Remote Work Visa accepts passive income as qualifying proof
    No (active foreign employment/contract required)
    Requires employment by/contract with a company outside South Africa, authorized under Section 11(1)(b)(iv) of the Immigration Act — the same structural shape as Belize's/Colombia's digital-nomad visas. Resolved 2026-07-18: fragomen.com (an immigration law firm) states plainly the route is "designed for active remote workers earning foreign income rather than those relying on passive income sources" and visa holders may not perform services for a South African entity — passive/investment income does NOT satisfy this route's own income test, distinct from the Retired Person's Visa below, which does accept it. South Africa's Department of Home Affairs (DHA)'s own PDF confirmed to exist and was directly fetched, though its automated text extraction did not surface this specific clause in readable form this pass — the value above rests primarily on fragomen.com/citizenremote.com's cross-corroborated reading of the same DHA framework, not a directly-quoted DHA sentence
  • Remote Work Visa duration / renewal
    12 months initially, renewable annually up to 3 years total
    A real working multi-year yes, not a one-shot 6-month permit the way Belize's equivalent runs
  • Remote Work Visa income threshold
    39500 $/year (ZAR 650,976)
    Launched 2024, still active. Provable via 3-6mo bank statements, employment contract, or business-income proof; requires health insurance, medical/radiological reports, police clearance from every country lived 12+ months over the last 5 years
  • Remote Work Visa tax-residency trigger
    183 days/12-month period
    If a Double Taxation Agreement exists and stay is under 183 days/12mo, may be tax-exempt; over 183 days triggers SA tax residency + registration with the South African Revenue Service (SARS). SARS's domain confirmed live 2026-07-18 (its provisional-tax page fetched directly for the income-viability facts below) but this specific 183-day physical-presence-test page was not independently re-located this pass — root-domain citation, medium confidence
  • Retired Person's Visa — mandatory private health insurance
    Comprehensive private health insurance valid in South Africa for the visa's full duration required; travel insurance explicitly not accepted
    A real compounding gate on this project's most-likely-fit visa route — not age-based (see the age-discrimination-ban row above), but no source found this pass confirms whether the required policy itself carries any age ceiling. Additional citations: citizenremote.com, ibn.co.za
  • Retired Person's Visa accepts passive income as qualifying proof
    Yes (rental income, dividends, and interest from investments are all explicitly confirmed acceptable, combinable with each other and with pension payments — not limited to a formal pension/annuity instrument)
    RESOLVED 2026-07-18 — this had been flagged as the single most important open thread in this file set since the 2026-07-11 first pass. ibn.co.za states directly, of South Africa's Department of Home Affairs (DHA): "the DHA accepts a wide range of passive income types totalling at least ZAR 37,000 per month, and you can combine multiple sources to reach that number... including rental income, dividends, interest from investments, and pension payments." Cross-corroborated by the South African Embassy in Argentina's own "Retired Person Visa - Section 20" PDF (embajadasudafrica.org.ar), which independently confirms "combined assets" (no minimum value) realising the required monthly income qualify — though that PDF's own text could not be fully machine-extracted this pass, so it counts as corroborating rather than as the primary citation. In practice, Home Affairs looks for roughly R8.88M in liquid net worth generating R37,000/month at a ~5% assumed yield — a useful planning benchmark, not a stated legal minimum
  • Retired Person's Visa converts to permanent residency
    Yes (conditional on the ZAR 37,000/month income being guaranteed for the applicant's lifetime — a lifelong state pension or irrevocable annuity specifically, per Immigration Act Section 27(e); an ordinary non-lifelong passive-income proof doesn't clear this specific gate even if it clears the visa's own income test)
    The temporary Retired Person's Visa itself now confirmed to accept ordinary passive income (rental/dividends/interest, see the accepts_passive_income row above) — but this specific permanent-residency (PR)-conversion gate's own "lifelong" requirement was not independently re-verified against South Africa's Department of Home Affairs (DHA)'s own text this pass, so divergence_flag stays Not yet checked rather than being upgraded alongside the sibling row

    2000 $/month (ZAR 37,000)
    From a pension, irrevocable annuity, retirement account, or "combined assets" realising that monthly income (now confirmed to include rental/dividends/interest — see the accepts_passive_income row); issued up to 4 years, fully renewable if income still provable

Red flags

The hard truths, stated plainly — real risks, sitting right next to everything that's actually going well.

  • Alcohol law
    Legal drinking age 18 (a Liquor Amendment Bill proposing 21 is a live, not-yet-law proposal); sales-hours restrictions vary by province
    Western Cape specifically restricts/bans Sunday retail sales outside bars/hotels; public drinking illegal. Root-domain citation only (ewn.co.za) — the specific article backing this row was not re-verified against a live URL this pass, flagged for follow-up rather than left uncited
  • Cannabis law
    Legal for private adult use since 2024's Cannabis for Private Purposes Act; draft regulations published 2026-02-02/03 propose up to 750g possession and 5-plant cultivation cap, public comment closed 2026-03-05, not yet finalized
    Sale/retail/commercial trade remains prohibited except licensed medical operators. One of the more permissive cannabis regimes found in this project — genuinely distinct from Thailand's post-2025 rollback. Primary source: Ministry of Justice and Constitutional Development's own media statement, cross-corroborated with iol.co.za, news24.com, and businessday.co.za's 2026-02 coverage of the same draft regulations
  • Currency / foreign-investment posture
    Rand strengthened from ~R17.25/USD to ~R16.40/USD since early 2025; SARB cut its repo rate by 100bps over 2025 (a further 25bps cut to 6.75% at the November 2025 meeting)
    Now quantified rather than a directional claim alone — the 2026-07-11 pass's unquantified "appreciation to new heights" framing is superseded by this specific rand move and repo-rate trend. Cross-corroborated with africacapitalwatch.com and disruptionbanking.com's independent 2026 rand-outlook coverage, and South African Reserve Bank's (SARB) own exchange-control-circular publication activity (resbank.co.za) confirming continued capital-flow-management liberalization in 2026
  • Exchange control regime for foreign nationals and immigrants (SARB primary text)
    Foreign nationals temporarily resident in South Africa may, once foreign assets are declared on arrival: bank on a resident basis, freely invest/dispose of foreign assets, hold Non-resident Rand or foreign-currency accounts, and transfer accumulated South African funds abroad if the source is substantiated and the amount is reasonable relative to South African income-generating activity. Immigrants (permanent residents) get a parallel track: free disposal of foreign assets from arrival, and within 5 years of immigrating, the right to retransfer/re-export all introduced assets including growth. Resident South Africans have a R1 million/calendar-year single discretionary allowance plus a separate R10 million/calendar-year foreign capital allowance
    Closes the "direct SARB page fetch" gap flagged since 2026-07-18 ( close-out, bonus find beyond the row's own DHA scope). The document's own version-control table shows 69 revisions since 2016, most recent 2026-01-07 — a pattern of continuous incremental liberalization, not tightening or freezing. This specific guideline does NOT cover property-purchase exchange-control mechanics (e.g. the widely-reported "foreigner may finance up to 50% of a SA property purchase via local mortgage" rule) — that appears to live in a separate SARB Authorised Dealer Manual/circular, not yet directly fetched; real, narrower remaining gap, though a financing-detail question this project's own conventions treat as lower research priority than cash-purchase mechanics generally (see [on file]'s mortgage/financing-detail note). divergence_flag left at Not yet checked: no first-hand account of a foreign national actually executing one of these transfers was found this pass — the mechanism is primary-sourced, lived practice is not yet confirmed

    10-party coalition led by ANC (40.2% national vote) + DA (21.8%); ANC lost majority for the first time since 1994 (29 May 2024 election). Full coalition: ANC, DA, Patriotic Alliance, Inkatha Freedom Party, Good Party, Pan Africanist Congress of Azania, Freedom Front Plus, United Democratic Movement, Al Jama-ah, Rise Mzansi
    Real friction over tax/land/education policy, described as friction not rupture; 2026 local government elections named as a real pressure point, with all coalition parties under pressure to demonstrate governing competence beforehand.   • Pet import (cat) — species-specific requirement

    A fifth genuine exception to this project's dog-only pet-import gap (both species researched and explicitly diverging) — cats face a meaningfully lighter process than dogs here. Pets can only enter as manifest cargo, not checked baggage or in-cabin. Primary source: National Department of Agriculture's (nda.agric.za) own import protocol, directly fetched 2026-07-18, cross-corroborated with pettravel.com, petport.co.za, and vetsaglik.com
  • Pet import (dog) — disease-test / titer requirement
    No rabies titer required from any origin; dogs require negative tests for 5 diseases (babesia, ehrlichia/dirofilaria, borrelia, leishmania, trypanosoma evansi) within 30 days of export. Quarantine mandatory (minimum 14 days at an approved facility) except from a short list of rabies-free/low-risk origin countries
    Rabies vaccine required 30 days-12 months before travel, administered on or after microchip implantation. Import permit (DAFF/nda.agric.za) required, minimum 4 weeks' processing lead time — a real, dated planning constraint worth naming plainly rather than leaving vague.
